BLACKPINK’s Lisa has once again dominated headlines, but this time the buzz isn’t about chart numbers or dazzling choreography. A Syrian visual artist has publicly accused the star’s newest solo track of appropriating traditional designs without credit, prompting a swift response from Lisa on her own social platforms.

## Background

Lisa, born Lalisa Manoban in 1997, rose to global fame as the main dancer and rapper of BLACKPINK, the YG Entertainment girl group that debuted in 2016. Since then, the quartet has broken multiple records, from the fastest YouTube views for a K‑pop music video to topping the Billboard 200 with their 2022 album *Born Pink*. Lisa’s individual popularity skyrocketed with the 2021 solo single “Lalisa,” which amassed over 70 million YouTube views within 24 hours and secured a spot on the Billboard Hot 100.

Beyond her group activities, Lisa has become a fashion icon, collaborating with luxury brands such as Celine and Dior, and her influence extends to streaming platforms where her solo releases consistently rank high on Spotify’s Global Top 50. Her latest solo project, released earlier this month, was promoted across YouTube, Apple Music, and Spotify, with a visually striking music video that showcases a blend of futuristic aesthetics and Middle‑Eastern motifs.

The K‑pop industry has a long history of incorporating diverse cultural elements into its visuals, from traditional hanbok references to African-inspired beats. While many fans celebrate this global fusion, critics argue that proper acknowledgment and collaboration with source communities are essential to avoid cultural insensitivity.

## What Happened

On September 2, 2026, Syrian visual artist **Rami Al‑Hussein** posted a side‑by‑side comparison on Instagram, highlighting striking similarities between a traditional Syrian embroidery pattern and a graphic element featured in Lisa’s music video. Al‑Hussein claimed the pattern was lifted without permission, stating, “Our heritage is being commercialized without consent.” The post quickly gained traction, garnering thousands of comments and shares, and was amplified by several K‑pop fan accounts.

Lisa responded within hours via an Instagram story, posting a screenshot of the accusation and adding a brief statement: “I respect all cultures and never intend to offend. I’m looking into this matter.” She did not elaborate further, nor did YG Entertainment release an official comment at the time of writing.

The controversy sparked a broader conversation on Twitter and Reddit, with fans dividing into two camps: those defending Lisa’s artistic freedom and those demanding accountability for cultural appropriation. Music streaming platforms have not removed the video, and no legal actions have been announced.

## Why This Matters

The incident underscores a growing awareness among global audiences about cultural representation in K‑pop. As the genre continues to dominate Western charts—BLACKPINK’s *Born Pink* still sits in the top 10 of the Billboard 200—its artists are under increasing scrutiny for how they engage with non‑Korean cultural symbols. A similar controversy erupted in 2023 when a major K‑pop label faced backlash over a music video that featured Native American imagery without consultation.

For Lisa, the stakes are high. Her solo brand is built on a blend of Thai heritage (she is the first Thai K‑pop idol) and cutting‑edge fashion, and any perceived misstep could affect her marketability, especially in regions where cultural sensitivity is a legal concern. Moreover, the incident may influence YG Entertainment’s internal vetting processes, prompting stricter guidelines for visual concepts and collaborations with cultural experts.

From an industry perspective, the episode adds to the ongoing dialogue about intellectual property rights for traditional art forms. UNESCO has long advocated for the protection of intangible cultural heritage, and cases like this highlight the need for clear licensing pathways when artists wish to incorporate such motifs.

## What Fans Can Expect

While the controversy is still unfolding, fans can still stream Lisa’s new single on all major platforms—YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music. The official music video remains available on BLACKPINK’s YouTube channel, and the track is slated for inclusion in upcoming performances on *The Tonight Show* and a possible Asian tour stop later this year.

If the matter escalates, we may see an official statement from YG Entertainment clarifying the creative process behind the visuals, or perhaps a collaborative effort with Syrian artists to properly credit the source material. In the meantime, fan communities are organizing discussion threads to dissect the visual similarities and to advocate for respectful cultural exchange.
