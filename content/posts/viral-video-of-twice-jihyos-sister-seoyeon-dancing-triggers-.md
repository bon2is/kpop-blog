The internet lit up this week when a short clip of Seoyeon, the older sister of TWICE’s leader Jihyo, burst onto social media dancing to a catchy K‑pop beat. Within hours the video racked up millions of views on YouTube and TikTok, sparking a wave of memes, praise for her moves, and a surprising debate about HYBE’s newly announced fan‑engagement service, TUIDE.

## Background

TWICE, the nine‑member girl group that debuted under JYP Entertainment in 2015, has become one of the most recognizable acts in K‑pop history. With hits like “Cheer Up,” “TT,” and “Fancy,” the group has sold over 10 million records worldwide, topped the Billboard Global 200, and amassed a dedicated fanbase known as “ONCE.” Jihyo, born Park Ji‑hyun, serves as the group’s main vocalist and has been praised for her powerful stage presence and charismatic leadership. Outside of group activities, Jihyo has occasionally shared glimpses of her personal life, including family moments that fans adore.

Seoyeon, who is a few years older than Jihyo, has largely stayed out of the public eye—until a fan captured her dancing in a living‑room setting and posted the clip online. The video shows her effortlessly executing a choreography that resembles the style of a TWICE stage performance, complete with sharp footwork and a confident smile. While Seoyeon is not a professional dancer, her enthusiasm resonated with fans who saw a relatable, family‑centric side of the idol’s world.

At the same time, HYBE, the powerhouse behind BTS, SEVENTEEN, and ENHYPEN, announced a new platform called TUIDE (short for “Talent‑User Interaction Development Environment”). TUIDE aims to let fans interact directly with artists through exclusive video content, behind‑the‑scenes footage, and AI‑driven recommendations. The service is expected to launch later this year on major streaming platforms and will be integrated with HYBE’s existing ecosystem, including Weverse and the HYBE Labels app.

## What Happened

The viral clip was first uploaded to YouTube on September 24, 2026, by a fan account that labeled the video “Jihyo’s sister shows off her moves!” Within 24 hours, the video surpassed 2 million views, and the hashtag #SeoyeonDance began trending on Twitter and TikTok. Fans praised Seoyeon’s confidence, with many commenting, “Age is not a problem or excuse,” echoing a line from a later interview where Jihyo defended her sister’s participation in public media.

The reaction was not uniformly positive. Some netizens questioned whether the video was a calculated move by HYBE to generate buzz for TUIDE before its official launch. Critics argued that leveraging a family member’s casual dance could be seen as a marketing ploy, potentially exploiting personal moments for corporate gain. Others expressed concern about privacy, noting that Seoyeon had not consented to a formal promotional role.

HYBE responded in a brief statement posted on its official Weverse channel, emphasizing that the video was “a genuine, fan‑captured moment” and that the company respects the personal boundaries of all artists and their families. The statement also reiterated that TUIDE will focus on “authentic fan‑artist connections” and will not rely on forced content.

Meanwhile, Jihyo addressed the controversy during a live broadcast on V LIVE, saying, “My sister loves to dance, and I’m proud of her. This video was shared by a fan who wanted to spread joy, not to promote any product.” She added that while she supports HYBE’s new platform, she hopes fans can separate the personal video from corporate strategy.

## Why This Matters

The incident highlights the delicate balance K‑pop agencies must maintain between fan engagement and personal privacy. HYBE’s TUIDE is positioned as a next‑generation fan platform, promising deeper interaction through AI‑curated content and exclusive streams. However, the rapid spread of Seoyeon’s video shows how any seemingly innocuous moment can be amplified into a larger conversation about corporate intent.

From an industry perspective, the story underscores a growing trend: agencies are increasingly turning to family members, friends, and behind‑the‑scenes footage to humanize idols and keep fans hooked between comebacks. This strategy can boost platform sign‑ups and streaming numbers, but it also risks backlash if fans perceive exploitation. The mixed reaction to Seoyeon’s clip serves as a case study for other labels considering similar tactics.

Chart-wise, while the video itself does not affect music rankings, the buzz around TUIDE could indirectly influence streaming performance. If fans sign up for the platform to access exclusive content, it could drive higher play counts on Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube, potentially boosting upcoming TWICE releases on the Billboard Global 200 and Circle Chart.

## What Fans Can Expect

For fans eager to see more of Seoyeon—or any family‑related content—HYBE has hinted that TUIDE will include “family moments” as part of its exclusive library, but only with explicit consent. The platform is slated to launch on major streaming services such as YouTube Premium and Apple TV+ later this year, with a subscription model that bundles Weverse+ benefits.

In the meantime, the viral video remains available on YouTube, where it can be streamed for free. Fans who want to support TWICE’s upcoming activities should keep an eye on the group’s official channels for the next comeback, expected in early 2027, and watch for any TUIDE‑related teasers that may appear on Weverse or HYBE’s official website.
