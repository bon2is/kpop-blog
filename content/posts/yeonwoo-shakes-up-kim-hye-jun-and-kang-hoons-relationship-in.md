The latest stills from tvN’s upcoming romantic comedy **"My Bias, My Boss"** have set the internet ablaze. Actress **Yeonwoo** (Lee Da‑hee) makes a surprise appearance that threatens to upend the budding romance between **Kim Hye‑Jun**’s character Nam Da‑Reum and **Kang Hoon**’s CEO Lee Chan. Fans are already debating where to stream the series and what this twist means for the love triangle at the heart of the show.

## Background

"My Bias, My Boss" is an adaptation of the hit Korean webtoon **"My Bias, My Boss"** (내 바이어, 내 사장). The original comic, serialized on Naver Webtoon, quickly amassed a dedicated readership for its blend of office politics, idol‑fan culture, and a light‑hearted love triangle. The story follows **Nam Da‑Reum** (played by Kim Hye‑Jun), a young woman who lands a job at a large entertainment agency hoping to meet her longtime idol, **Lee Chan** (Cha Woo‑Min). Unbeknownst to her, Lee Chan is not just a pop star but also the charismatic CEO of the company she now works for.

Kim Hye‑Jun rose to fame as a member of the indie‑rock band **The Koxx** before transitioning to acting. Her recent drama credits include the thriller **"The Devil’s Deal"** and the romantic series **"Coffee, Do You Want Me?"**, showcasing her versatility across genres. Kang Hoon, meanwhile, earned a reputation as a “romantic lead” after starring in the 2022 hit **"Love in the Moonlight 2"** and the 2023 melodrama **"Echoes of Yesterday"**. Both actors have strong fanbases in the US, UK, and Canada, making any on‑screen chemistry a potential streaming draw.

The drama is slated to premiere on tvN in early October 2026, with global distribution handled by **Netflix** and **Viki**. Early promotional material suggests a 12‑episode run, each episode running roughly 60 minutes. For viewers outside Korea, the series will be available with subtitles on Netflix’s “Asian TV” hub and on Viki’s “K‑Drama” section, both of which are popular platforms for Western K‑Drama fans.

## What Happened

On September 28, tvN released a batch of new stills on its official Instagram and the drama’s website. The most talked‑about image features **Yeonwoo**—known for her breakout role in **"Reply 1994"** and as a former member of the girl group **Wonder Girls**—standing beside Lee Chan (Cha Woo‑Min) in a sleek boardroom. The caption reads, “A new player enters the game,” hinting at a plot twist that could shift the series’ romantic dynamics.

According to a statement from the production team, Yeonwoo’s character is **“a senior executive who holds a secret connection to Lee Chan’s past.”** The cameo is not just a stunt; it’s a pivotal narrative device that will test Da‑Reum’s loyalty and push the love triangle into uncharted territory. In a press release, director **Lee Jae‑Yong** said, “Yeonwoo brings a fresh energy that will challenge both our protagonists and keep viewers guessing until the final episode.”

The drama’s official trailer, released two weeks earlier, already hinted at the tension between Da‑Reum and Lee Chan, but the new stills confirm that the series will delve deeper into corporate intrigue and personal histories. The production also announced that the series will be available for streaming on **Netflix** worldwide **four weeks after the Korean broadcast**, aligning with the platform’s strategy to capture binge‑watching audiences in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia.

## Why This Matters

The inclusion of a high‑profile cameo like Yeonwoo signals a growing trend in K‑Drama production: leveraging established idols and veteran actors to boost domestic ratings and international streaming numbers. Yeonwoo’s fanbase spans multiple continents, and her involvement is likely to drive additional viewership on Netflix, where K‑Drama subscriptions have surged by 27% year‑over‑year according to a 2025 Nielsen report.

From an industry perspective, “My Bias, My Boss” is part of tvN’s broader strategy to adapt popular webtoons—a formula that has yielded global hits such as **"Itaewon Class"** and **"Sweet Home"**. By blending the webtoon’s established fanbase with star power, the drama aims to climb the **Billboard Global 200** streaming charts for TV series, a metric that now includes major streaming platforms like Netflix and Viki.

Moreover, the drama’s premise—an ordinary employee navigating a romance with a celebrity‑CEO—taps into the cultural fascination with “idol‑fan” dynamics, a theme that resonates strongly with Western audiences who consume K‑Pop and K‑Drama simultaneously. This cross‑genre appeal could position the series for awards consideration at the **Asian Television Awards** and increase its visibility on platforms that track viewership data, such as **Mubi’s Top 10 TV** list.

## What Fans Can Expect

With the new stills out, fans are eagerly anticipating how Yeonwoo’s character will influence the storyline. Early speculation suggests a possible love‑triangle expansion where Da‑Reum must choose between her idol‑CEO and the mysterious executive, adding layers of corporate espionage and personal redemption. The drama’s writers have promised “unexpected twists” and “heart‑warming moments,” so viewers can look forward to emotional payoff in the mid‑season climax.

In addition to the main plot, the series will feature original OST tracks from popular K‑Pop groups, slated for release on **Spotify**, **Apple Music**, and **YouTube Music** concurrently with each episode. The lead single, performed by **(G)I‑DLE**, is expected to chart on the **Circle Digital Chart** and may even break into the **Billboard Hot 100**, echoing the success of previous drama‑OST crossovers.

For those eager to watch, the first episode will air on tvN at 21:00 KST on October 5, 2026, and will be uploaded to Netflix on November 2, 2026. International fans can set reminders on Netflix’s “My List” feature to ensure they don’t miss the premiere.

💭 **Editor’s Take:** As a long‑time follower of both K‑Drama and K‑Pop, I’m thrilled to see “My Bias, My Boss” push the envelope with Yeonwoo’s surprise cameo. It feels like a strategic masterstroke—mixing idol nostalgia with fresh corporate intrigue. The love‑triangle setup isn’t new, but the added layer of a secret‑linked executive promises the kind of dramatic tension that keeps binge‑watchers glued to their screens. I’m also excited about the soundtrack; if (G)I‑DLE’s track lands on the Hot 100, it could be the next big crossover moment that solidifies the symbiotic relationship between Korean dramas and global pop music.
