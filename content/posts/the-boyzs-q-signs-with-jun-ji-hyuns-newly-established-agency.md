THE BOYZ’s Q, real name Min‑seok Son, has just taken a bold step beyond music by signing with PEARLY Company, the freshly minted talent agency launched by Hollywood‑kissed Korean star Jun Ji‑Hyun. The announcement, confirmed on September 4, promises dedicated acting support for the 23‑year‑old idol, sparking excitement among fans who’ve long hoped to see him shine on the small screen.

## Background

THE BOYZ debuted in 2017 under Cre.ker Entertainment and quickly rose to fame with hits like “D.D.D,” “The Stealer,” and the chart‑topping “The Stealer” that earned them a spot on Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales chart. Known for their powerful choreography and tight vocal harmonies, the ten‑member group has cultivated a loyal global fanbase, especially after their 2022 comeback “The Piece” broke into the Circle Album Chart’s top 5.

Member Q, born Son Min‑seok on August 16, 1999, entered the industry as a trainee at age 14 and trained for five years before debuting. While primarily a vocalist and dancer, Q has always shown an interest in acting, appearing in cameo roles on the web drama “The Witch’s House” (2021) and the music‑driven series “Love & Hate” (2022). His on‑screen charisma earned him a reputation as a “triple threat” among fans, but until now he lacked a dedicated agency to nurture that side of his career.

Jun Ji‑Hyun, a household name thanks to iconic films like “My Sassy Girl” and dramas such as “My Love from the Star,” launched PEACHY Company in 2023 with the goal of cultivating versatile talent across acting, modeling, and music. The agency’s roster already includes rising actors Lee Jae‑won and Park Sa‑e‑ri, but Q will be its first idol‑turned‑actor, marking a strategic expansion into the K‑pop talent pool.

## What Happened

On September 4, PEACHY Company issued an official statement on its website: “It’s true that we have signed an exclusive contract with Q of THE BOYZ. We plan to fully support Q’s activities as an actor.” The agency emphasized that Q will receive tailored acting workshops, audition opportunities, and a dedicated management team focused on building his filmography. No details about a specific drama or film have been released yet, but insiders suggest a “high‑profile” Korean drama slated for early 2025 could be in the works.

The news broke on Soompi and quickly spread across Twitter, with fans using the hashtag #QxPEACHY trending worldwide. In a brief interview with a Korean entertainment outlet, Jun Ji‑Hyun praised Q’s “raw talent and sincere passion for acting,” noting that the agency’s mission is to “bridge the gap between idol performance and serious acting.” Q, for his part, posted a heartfelt thank‑you message on Instagram, expressing gratitude to his fans and promising to “work hard and make you proud.”

## Why This Matters

Q’s signing is significant on several fronts. First, it underscores a growing trend of idols transitioning into acting under specialized agencies rather than staying with their original music labels. While many idols have taken on acting gigs, few have secured exclusive contracts with agencies that prioritize acting development. This move could set a precedent for other idols seeking serious acting careers.

Second, Jun Ji‑Hyun’s involvement adds star power and credibility. Her reputation for selecting quality projects means Q will likely receive roles that challenge him, rather than typical idol cameo appearances. This could elevate his acting résumé and, by extension, boost THE BOYZ’s overall brand visibility in drama‑centric markets like Japan and Southeast Asia.

Finally, the partnership highlights the increasingly symbiotic relationship between K‑pop and Korean drama industries. As Korean content continues to dominate global streaming platforms—Netflix, Disney+, and Prime Video report record viewership for Korean series—having a foothold in both worlds maximizes an artist’s international reach. Q’s future projects could appear on these platforms, driving cross‑promotional opportunities for THE BOYZ’s music releases.

## What Fans Can Expect

In the coming months, fans should watch for casting announcements featuring Q, especially on major Korean broadcasting networks (MBC, SBS, KBS) and streaming services like Netflix Korea or Disney+ Asia. PEACHY Company has hinted at “strategic collaborations” with production houses, so a drama with a strong ensemble cast is plausible. Additionally, Q may appear in promotional teasers or behind‑the‑scenes content on YouTube, giving fans a glimpse of his preparation process.

On the music side, THE BOYZ are slated to begin preparations for their next album in early 2025, according to recent Cre.ker interviews. Q’s acting schedule will be coordinated to avoid conflicts, but fans can anticipate a possible dual‑promotion period where a drama OST featuring Q’s vocals could be released, echoing successful cross‑overs like IU’s “Palette” or Baekhyun’s drama soundtracks.
