The latest stills from Disney+’s highly anticipated sequel "Made in Korea 2" have hit the internet, and they’re sparking serious buzz. Veteran actors Hyun Bin and Jung Woo‑sung lock eyes in a tense showdown, while rising star Woo Do‑hwan appears trapped between their opposing ambitions. The images promise a darker, more complex narrative than the first season, and fans are already scrambling to find out where and when they can stream the drama.

## Background

"Made in Korea" debuted on Disney+ in early 2023, quickly becoming one of the platform’s flagship Korean originals. The series follows Baek Ki‑tae (Hyun Bin), a ruthless businessman whose single‑minded pursuit of wealth and power puts him at odds with prosecutor Jang Geon‑young (Jung Woo‑sung). The cat‑and‑mouse dynamic between the two, set against a backdrop of corporate corruption and legal intrigue, resonated with both domestic and international audiences. The first season earned a solid 7.8 rating on IMDb, topped the Netflix Top 10 in South Korea for several weeks despite being on Disney+, and sparked conversation on Billboard’s “World Albums” chart as its original soundtrack climbed into the top 10.

Hyun Bin, best known for his breakout role in "Crash Landing on You" and the 2022 hit "The Veil," has become a global A‑list star, with his recent appearance on the cover of Variety’s “Power of the Year” issue. Jung Woo‑sung, meanwhile, earned critical acclaim for his performances in "The King" and the Netflix drama "Hospital Playlist 2," cementing his reputation as one of Korea’s most versatile actors. Woo Do‑hwan, a younger talent recognized for his supporting turn in the 2021 drama "Vincenzo," is gaining momentum and is expected to become a household name after this project.

The original series was praised for its high production values, cinematic cinematography, and a soundtrack that blended traditional Korean instruments with modern EDM—an approach that helped the OST chart on the Billboard World Digital Song Sales. Its success encouraged Disney+ to green‑light a sequel, positioning the series as a flagship for the platform’s expanding Korean content library.

## What Happened

On August 22, Disney+ released a set of new promotional stills from "Made in Korea 2," revealing the visual tone of the sequel. The images depict Hyun Bin’s character—now a more seasoned, scar‑marked mogul—standing in a sleek boardroom, eyes narrowed as he confronts Jung Woo‑sung’s prosecutor, who is shown with a determined, almost defiant stare. Between them, Woo Do‑hwan’s character appears visibly uneasy, clutching a file folder that hints at incriminating evidence.

The official Disney+ press release confirmed that the sequel will continue the story of power, betrayal, and redemption, but will shift focus to a new corporate scandal involving a tech conglomerate. Hyun Bin’s Baek Ki‑tae is now the CEO of a multinational venture, while Jung Woo‑sung’s Jang Geon‑young has been promoted to chief anti‑corruption investigator. Woo Do‑hwan’s role is described as “the pivotal whistle‑blower whose loyalty will decide the fate of both titans.”

No exact release date has been announced yet, but Disney+ has indicated that the series will drop in the fourth quarter of 2026, with a global rollout on the platform. The press release also mentioned that the original soundtrack will feature collaborations with international artists, a move that could push the songs onto Billboard’s Hot 100 and Spotify’s Global Top 50.

## Why This Matters

"Made in Korea 2" represents a strategic push by Disney+ to capture the lucrative K‑Drama market, which has surged in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia. According to a 2025 Nielsen report, Korean scripted content now accounts for 12 % of all streaming minutes in the U.S., second only to domestic drama series. By securing top‑tier talent like Hyun Bin and Jung Woo‑sung, Disney+ signals its commitment to competing with Netflix, which dominates the Korean‑original space with hits like "Squid Game" and "All of Us Are Dead."

The series also underscores the growing importance of cross‑border star power. Hyun Bin’s recent cameo in a Hollywood action film and Jung Woo‑sung’s appearance at the Cannes Film Festival have expanded their fanbases beyond Asia. Their involvement draws in both K‑Drama enthusiasts and western viewers who follow their international projects, boosting Disney+ subscriber growth in key markets.

From an industry standpoint, the sequel’s focus on a tech conglomerate mirrors real‑world concerns about data privacy and corporate monopoly in South Korea. By weaving contemporary issues into its narrative, the show positions itself as culturally relevant, a factor that has helped previous Korean dramas dominate the Billboard 200’s soundtrack category.

## What Fans Can Expect

Fans should anticipate a darker visual palette, more intricate plot twists, and a deeper exploration of moral ambiguity. The promotional stills suggest that the boardroom battles will be matched with high‑octane chase sequences, hinting at a blend of legal thriller and action drama. The series is also expected to feature cameo appearances from other notable Korean actors, though details remain under wraps.

The soundtrack will likely be a major talking point. Disney+ has teased that global EDM producer Zedd and K‑pop idol IU will each contribute a track, raising the possibility of the OST breaking into the Billboard Hot 100. Additionally, the series will be available with multi‑language subtitles, ensuring accessibility for non‑Korean speakers.

Woo Do‑hwan’s role as the reluctant whistle‑blower adds a fresh layer of suspense that could make "Made in Korea 2" a must‑watch for anyone who loves a good thriller with a Korean twist.
