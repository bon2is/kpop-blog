NiziU has just turned up the heat on their global fanbase. The J‑pop‑K‑pop hybrid girl group unveiled a cinematic prologue film for their upcoming Korean mini‑album **Sour Grapes**, and confirmed the release date—September 28 at 6 p.m. KST. The teaser, packed with bold visuals and a haunting synth‑driven hook, has already sparked massive buzz across YouTube and Twitter, with fans scrambling to set reminders on streaming platforms.

## Background

Formed through the 2020 reality show *Nizi Project*, NiziU quickly rose to prominence under JYP Entertainment’s guidance, blending J‑pop sensibilities with K‑pop’s polished production. The nine‑member lineup—Mako, Rio, Maya, Riku, Ayaka, Mayuka, Rima, Miihi, and Nina—debuted in June 2020 with the single “Make You Happy,” which topped Japan’s Oricon and Billboard Japan charts. Their debut EP *Make You Happy* sold over 400,000 copies in its first week, earning a Platinum certification from the Recording Industry Association of Japan (RIAJ).

Since then, NiziU has consistently delivered chart‑topping hits such as “Step and a Step” and “Take a Picture,” both of which broke streaming records on Spotify Japan and secured spots on the Billboard Global 200. Their 2022 Japanese album *U* debuted at No. 1 on the Oricon Albums Chart and entered the Billboard 200 at # 84, a rare feat for a Japanese‑language act. While the group’s primary activities have centered on the Japanese market, they have maintained a growing presence on global platforms like YouTube, TikTok, and Apple Music, amassing over 10 million subscribers on their official channel.

The members have also ventured into Korean media, appearing on variety shows and collaborating with Korean artists, which has cultivated a bilingual fanbase eager for a full‑scale Korean comeback. Their last Korean‑language release was the 2021 digital single “Poppin’ Love,” a limited‑edition track that hinted at the group’s cross‑cultural ambitions. The upcoming mini‑album marks NiziU’s first Korean‑language project since that teaser, and it arrives at a time when J‑pop acts are increasingly targeting the global K‑pop market.

## What Happened

On September 7, at exactly midnight KST, NiziU’s official social media channels posted a striking teaser image—a dark, grape‑vine‑laden set bathed in neon red light—accompanied by the caption “Sour Grapes – 2024‑09‑28.” The same day, a 30‑second prologue film premiered on YouTube, showcasing each member in stylized, moody vignettes that blend street‑wear aesthetics with avant‑garde choreography. The background track, an unreleased snippet of the title song, features a pulsating bass line and a haunting chorus that hints at a more mature, experimental sound compared to their bright, bubble‑gum pop origins.

The announcement also confirmed that the mini‑album will contain five tracks, including the lead single “Sour Grapes,” a B‑side titled “Midnight Bloom,” and three other songs yet to be titled. JYP Entertainment released a brief statement: “NiziU is ready to explore new flavors and emotions with ‘Sour Grapes.’ This project showcases the members’ growth as artists and will be available worldwide on all major streaming platforms.” The group’s official website now lists pre‑order links for both physical and digital editions, with limited‑edition photobooks and a QR code that unlocks exclusive behind‑the‑scenes footage.

While specific chart projections are not yet available, industry insiders predict a strong debut on the Circle Album Chart (formerly Gaon) and anticipate significant streaming numbers on Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube. The lead single is expected to be promoted on Korean music shows such as *M Countdown* and *Music Bank*, and a choreography teaser is slated for release a week before the album drop, further fueling anticipation.

## Why This Matters

NiziU’s Korean comeback is a strategic milestone for several reasons. First, it underscores JYP Entertainment’s continued push to position its Japanese‑focused acts within the broader K‑pop ecosystem, following successful cross‑market ventures by groups like TWICE and Stray Kids. By releasing a Korean mini‑album, NiziU can tap into the lucrative Korean music show circuit, which provides exposure to domestic audiences and boosts eligibility for year‑end awards such as the Golden Disc Awards and Mnet Asian Music Awards.

Second, the timing aligns with a growing appetite among Western fans for multilingual K‑pop content. Recent data from Spotify shows that Korean‑language tracks dominate the “Top 50 Global” playlist, and platforms like YouTube Music and Apple Music have dedicated K‑pop sections that feature both Korean and Japanese releases. A Korean‑language project from NiziU expands their discoverability on these platforms, potentially driving higher chart placements on the Billboard Global 200 and even the Hot 100 if the lead single garners strong digital sales and streaming.

Finally, the artistic direction hinted at in the trailer suggests a shift toward a more mature, genre‑blending sound. This evolution mirrors the trajectory of other second‑generation groups who have reinvented themselves to stay relevant—think BTS’s “Map of the Soul” era or BLACKPINK’s collaboration‑heavy releases. If NiziU can successfully navigate this transition, they may secure a longer‑term foothold in the competitive K‑pop market, beyond their current status as a Japanese‑centric act.

## What Fans Can Expect

Fans should prepare for a multi‑platform rollout. The mini‑album will be available for streaming on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, and YouTube Music globally on September 28 at 6 p.m. KST. Physical copies—including standard, limited, and photobook editions—will ship worldwide through JYP’s official store and major retailers like YesAsia and Amazon. A music video for the title track “Sour Grapes” is slated for release on the same day, likely premiering on YouTube and promoted via the group’s TikTok and Instagram Reels channels.

In the weeks leading up to the comeback, NiziU will likely appear on Korean variety shows and conduct a series of live‑streamed fan meetings on platforms such as V‑Live (now merged with Weverse) and TikTok. The group may also schedule a short promotional showcase broadcast live on YouTube, offering international fans a chance to watch the debut performance in real time.
