Tomorrow X Together (TXT) set fans’ hearts racing on August 27 at midnight KST when they unveiled the official schedule for their 2026‑2027 world tour, titled **"Steal The Wind."** The announcement not only confirmed three back‑to‑back concerts in Seoul from November 13‑15, 2026, but also revealed an ambitious itinerary that will span five continents, hitting major markets such as Los Angeles, London, and Tokyo. With ticket sales expected to open soon on platforms like Ticketmaster and Interpark, the hype is already palpable among the group’s global fanbase.

## Background

Formed by Big Hit Music (now part of HYBE) in 2019, TXT quickly rose from rookie status to become one of K‑Pop’s most versatile acts. Their debut EP *The Dream Chapter: STAR* topped South Korea’s Gaon Album Chart and earned them a rookie award at the 2020 Mnet Asian Music Awards. Since then, the five‑member unit—Soobin, Yeonjun, Beomgyu, Taehyun, and Huening Kai—has consistently pushed musical boundaries, blending synth‑pop, rock, and R&B across releases like *The Chaos Chapter: FREEZE* (2021) and *The Name Chapter: TEMPTATION* (2023).

TXT’s recent discography has cemented their place on international charts. The lead single “Good Boy Gone Bad” debuted at #12 on Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales, while the full album *The Name Chapter: TEMPTATION* entered the Billboard 200 at #41, a rare feat for a Korean act in their early years. Their growing presence on streaming services—Spotify’s “Global Top 50” and Apple Music’s “K‑Pop Spotlight”—demonstrates a fanbase that stretches far beyond Korea.

The group’s previous world tour, *“Tomorrow X Together 2022–2023: LILAC,”* sold out arenas in North America and Europe, proving their live‑performance draw. That tour also marked the first time TXT performed at a stadium in Seoul, hinting at the scale they now aim for with “Steal The Wind.”

## What Happened

During a midnight livestream on YouTube and Weverse, TXT revealed a detailed itinerary for the upcoming tour. After the Seoul kickoff (Nov 13‑15, 2026), the itinerary continues with stops in:

- **North America:** Los Angeles (Dec 3), New York (Dec 7), Chicago (Dec 10), and Toronto (Dec 14).
- **Europe:** London (Jan 5, 2027), Paris (Jan 9), Berlin (Jan 12), and Madrid (Jan 16).
- **Asia (outside Korea):** Tokyo (Feb 2, 2027), Bangkok (Feb 6), Singapore (Feb 10), and Manila (Feb 14).
- **Oceania:** Sydney (Mar 1, 2027) and Auckland (Mar 4, 2027).
- **South America:** São Paulo (Mar 12, 2027) and Buenos Aires (Mar 16, 2027).

Each city will be hosted in a stadium‑type venue, signaling a step up from the arena shows of the previous tour. The group also announced a limited‑edition “Steal The Wind” merch line, available exclusively through the official TXT store and partnered retailers like Hot Topic in the U.S.

In the livestream, leader Soobin said, “We’ve always wanted to share our music with the world in a space that feels as big as our dreams. ‘Steal The Wind’ is our promise to bring that energy to every fan, no matter where you are.” The announcement was accompanied by a teaser video featuring aerial shots of iconic landmarks—Eiffel Tower, Statue of Liberty, and Sydney Opera House—intercut with snippets of the new single “Wind Whisper.”

Ticket sales are slated to begin on September 5 via Ticketmaster (North America), Live Nation (Europe), and Interpark (Asia). Pre‑sale codes will be distributed to fans who have purchased the “Steal The Wind” pre‑order album on platforms such as Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music, encouraging early engagement.

## Why This Matters

The scale of “Steal The Wind” underscores TXT’s rapid ascent from a rookie group to stadium‑headliners within just four years. In the competitive K‑Pop landscape, only a handful of acts—BTS, BLACKPINK, SEVENTEEN—have consistently sold out stadiums worldwide. TXT’s ability to secure venues like Los Angeles Stadium (capacity 70,000) and London’s Wembley Arena (capacity 12,500) signals both strong domestic support and growing international demand.

From an industry perspective, the tour aligns with HYBE’s broader strategy to globalize its roster. By pairing TXT’s tour with simultaneous releases on major streaming platforms (the new single will drop on Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music the same day as the Seoul shows), HYBE maximizes cross‑market exposure. The tour also arrives at a time when K‑Pop concert attendance is rebounding post‑pandemic, with Billboard reporting a 38% increase in ticket sales for Asian acts in 2023‑24.

Chart-wise, the upcoming single “Wind Whisper” is expected to make a splash on the Billboard Hot 100 and Circle Digital Chart, especially given the promotional push surrounding the tour. If the track mirrors the streaming performance of “Good Boy Gone Bad,” it could break into the top 30 on the Hot 100, further solidifying TXT’s status as a global chart contender.

## What Fans Can Expect

Beyond the massive venues, fans can anticipate an immersive concert experience. The production team promises a “wind‑themed” stage design, featuring kinetic LED walls, real‑time wind‑simulation effects, and augmented‑reality visuals that sync with each song’s narrative. Setlists are rumored to blend classic hits like “Crown” and “Blue Hour” with new tracks from the upcoming EP, slated for release in early 2027.

In addition to the main shows, TXT will host intimate “Fan‑Meeting” events in select cities, offering photo‑ops, Q&A sessions, and exclusive merch bundles. These events will be livestreamed on YouTube and V Live for fans unable to attend in person, ensuring global accessibility.

For those eager to stream the concert later, HYBE has hinted at a partnership with Netflix for a documentary‑style concert film, potentially releasing in late 2027. Meanwhile, the live audio will be available on Spotify’s “Live at Stadiums” playlist shortly after each show.

## 💭 Editor's Take

💭 Editor's Take: As a longtime K‑Pop journalist, watching TXT evolve from the “boys next door” image of *The Dream Chapter* to stadium‑size performers feels like witnessing a coming‑of‑age story in real time. “Steal The Wind” isn’t just a tour—it’s a declaration that the fifth‑generation group is ready to claim their place on the world stage. The blend of ambitious production, strategic streaming tie‑ins, and fan‑centric events shows HYBE’s savvy in turning a concert series into a multi‑platform experience. If you’ve ever wondered where to watch the next big K‑Pop spectacle, keep an eye on the September ticket drop and be ready to stream the live concert on YouTube or catch the eventual Netflix documentary. This tour could very well be the moment TXT cements their legacy alongside the genre’s megastars.
