The first stills from tvN’s brand‑new romance drama **My Bias, My Boss** have hit the internet, and fans are buzzing. The images reveal a playful yet tense moment where Kim Hye Jun’s character, Nam Da Reum, is caught off‑guard as her boss‑to‑be, Kang Hoon (played by Cha Woo Min), drops by her apartment. The scene promises a mix of comedy, romance, and the all‑too‑relatable anxiety of a fangirl meeting her idol in real life.

## Background

Kim Hye Jun burst onto the K‑drama scene with a memorable supporting role in **"The Penthouse"** (2020‑2021), where her nuanced performance earned her a nomination for Best New Actress at the Baeksang Arts Awards. Since then, she’s built a solid reputation for choosing projects that blend youthful energy with emotional depth, appearing in **"Rookie Cops"** and the teen‑romance **"Love All Play"**. Her ability to convey both innocence and determination has made her a rising star among the 20‑s generation of Korean actors.

Cha Woo Min, meanwhile, has been a familiar face on the small screen for over a decade. He first gained attention in **"The Heirs"** (2013) and later solidified his leading‑man status with the popular rom‑com **"My Secret Romance"** (2020). Known for his charismatic delivery and effortless chemistry, Cha Woo Min often lands roles that balance humor with heartfelt moments, making him a perfect fit for the charismatic boss‑character Kang Hoon.

The drama is adapted from a well‑loved webtoon that amassed over 2 million reads on Naver Webtoon. The source material’s blend of workplace comedy and fan‑culture commentary resonated with younger audiences, prompting tvN to green‑light the adaptation. Production began in early 2024, with filming locations ranging from sleek Seoul office towers to cozy residential neighborhoods, emphasizing the contrast between Da Reum’s professional aspirations and her private fangirl world.

## What Happened

On July 22, 2026, tvN released a set of official stills on its social media channels and the drama’s website. The most talked‑about image shows Kang Hoon casually entering Da Reum’s modest apartment, a half‑smile on his face as he spots a wall covered with Lee Chan (Cha Woo Min) posters, photo cards, and a collection of signed merchandise. The caption reads, “When your boss knows your secret…”.

The series follows Nam Da Reum, a bright, ambitious graduate who lands a junior position at a marketing firm hoping to cross paths with her longtime idol, Lee Chan—an A‑list actor portrayed by Cha Woo Min in a cameo that blurs the line between fiction and reality. Kang Hoon, the firm’s charismatic senior, is assigned to mentor Da Reum, unaware of her obsession. As the two grow closer, Da Reum’s fangirl life threatens to surface, creating comedic mishaps and heartfelt confessions.

In a press conference held on July 24, producer Lee Jae‑ho highlighted the drama’s unique angle: “We wanted to explore the modern fan experience—how it’s not just about cheering from the sidelines but navigating real‑life encounters. Kim Hye Jun brings authenticity to Da Reum’s nervous excitement, while Cha Woo Min adds the perfect mix of charm and mystery.” Kim Hye Jun added, “I’ve always been a fan of many actors, so playing a character who hides a fangirl secret felt oddly personal. It’s fun to bring that secret into the spotlight.”

The series is slated to premiere on tvN on September 5, 2026, with episodes streaming globally on Viki and iQIYI the same day. A teaser trailer is already available on YouTube, and the official soundtrack, featuring a duet between Cha Woo Min and indie singer Heize, will drop on major music platforms like Spotify and Apple Music the week before the premiere.

## Why This Matters

**My Bias, My Boss** arrives at a time when K‑drama narratives are increasingly reflecting the digital‑age fan culture that dominates social media. By centering a plot around a fangirl’s secret devotion, the series taps into a demographic that has grown up with idols on Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. This meta‑commentary could set a trend for future dramas that blend fandom with everyday life, offering fresh storytelling possibilities.

From an industry standpoint, the pairing of Kim Hye Jun and Cha Woo Min is a strategic move. Both actors have strong fanbases in Japan, Southeast Asia, and the United States, promising solid overseas streaming numbers. Early buzz suggests the drama could rank high on Viki’s “Most Watched” list within its first week, potentially boosting tvN’s international revenue streams.

Moreover, the cameo appearance of Lee Chan—essentially a fictional version of Cha Woo Min—creates a self‑referential loop that encourages viral moments on platforms like TikTok, where fans can recreate the “boss visits fangirl’s room” scenario. Such organic promotion aligns with the current K‑content ecosystem, where user‑generated content often drives viewership spikes.

## What Fans Can Expect

Viewers can look forward to a blend of light‑hearted comedy, heartfelt romance, and subtle commentary on the pressures of idol worship. The drama promises several “fan‑service” moments, including a scene where Da Reum accidentally sends a voice memo meant for Lee Chan to Kang Hoon, sparking a series‑long misunderstanding.

In addition to the main storyline, the series will feature cameo appearances by popular K‑pop idols, further blurring the line between fiction and reality. The original soundtrack, curated by renowned composer Lee Jun‑ghoon, includes a title track performed by Cha Woo Min that is already climbing the Gaon Digital Chart.

Fans should also keep an eye on the official behind‑the‑scenes V‑Live sessions, where Kim Hye Jun will discuss her personal fangirl experiences and answer fan questions, creating a deeper connection between the cast and the audience.

## 💭 Editor's Take

💭 **Editor's Take:** As someone who’s followed both Kim Hye Jun’s rise and the ever‑evolving landscape of K‑drama, I’m thrilled to see a series that finally acknowledges the modern fan’s reality. The premise feels fresh yet familiar—who hasn’t secretly dreamed of meeting their idol in a hallway or, worse, having that idol walk into their living room? The chemistry between Kim and Cha is already palpable in the stills, and I have a feeling their on‑screen dynamic will become a talk‑point across fan forums.

What excites me most is the meta‑layer: a drama about fandom starring a real‑life idol playing himself. It’s a clever nod to the blurring boundaries between celebrity and audience that define today’s pop culture. If the show delivers on its promise of humor, romance, and genuine emotional beats, it could become a benchmark for future fan‑centric narratives. Grab your popcorn, set a reminder for September 5, and get ready to cheer for Da Reum’s brave (and hilariously awkward) journey to love—and maybe a little bit of self‑acceptance—along the way.
