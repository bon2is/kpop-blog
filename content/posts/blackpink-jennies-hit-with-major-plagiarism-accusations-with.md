Jennie, the charismatic main rapper and vocalist of global girl group BLACKPINK, has ignited a firestorm after her latest solo single was accused of copying a previously released track. Within hours of the teaser dropping, fans and music critics flooded social media with side‑by‑side comparisons, demanding answers from her agency.

## Background

Since debuting with BLACKPINK in 2016 under YG Entertainment, Jennie Kim has become one of the most recognizable faces in K‑Pop. The group’s meteoric rise—marked by chart‑topping hits like “Ddu‑du Ddu‑du,” “Kill This Love,” and the record‑breaking YouTube debut of “How You Like That”—secured them a permanent spot on Billboard’s Global 200 and the coveted Circle Chart’s Artist of the Year title in 2022. Jennie's solo debut, “Solo,” released in 2018, shattered streaming records on Spotify and Apple Music, quickly earning a place on the Billboard Hot 100 and solidifying her reputation as a stand‑alone star.

In the years following, Jennie has dabbled in fashion collaborations, brand ambassadorships, and occasional soundtrack contributions, but a full‑scale solo comeback has been eagerly anticipated by fans worldwide. The hype surrounding her upcoming single—titled “Eclipse”—has been building since YG posted a cryptic teaser on Instagram last week, promising a “new era” for the artist.

## What Happened

On July 22, YG Entertainment released a 30‑second teaser for “Eclipse,” showcasing a moody, neon‑lit set and a snippet of the chorus. Within minutes, the clip was uploaded to YouTube, where it amassed over 2 million views in the first hour. However, the moment the chorus hit, listeners began pointing out striking similarities to “Midnight Sun,” a 2019 track by indie R&B duo Luna & The Sky, which was popular on Korean streaming platforms and had a modest presence on Spotify’s Global R&B playlist.

Netizens created comparison videos that highlighted identical chord progressions, melodic phrasing, and even a matching vocal riff. The hashtag #JenniePlagiarism trended on Twitter and Korean platform Naver, garnering over 150,000 mentions within 24 hours. Some users claimed the production team might have sampled the original without proper clearance, while others argued that the similarities were coincidental, noting that many K‑Pop songs share common pop structures.

YG responded with a brief statement on their official website, saying, “We are aware of the concerns raised by fans regarding the musical composition of ‘Eclipse.’ The company is conducting a thorough review and will provide an official update as soon as possible.” No further details about songwriters or producers were disclosed at the time of the statement.

## Why This Matters

Plagiarism accusations are not new to K‑Pop, but they carry heightened weight when they involve a high‑profile artist like Jennie. In 2020, the industry faced similar controversies when BTS’s “Dynamite” was compared to a 1970s disco track, prompting discussions about the fine line between inspiration and infringement. For Jennie, the stakes are even higher because her solo brand is still being defined separate from BLACKPINK’s massive collective identity.

From an industry standpoint, the controversy underscores the intense pressure on Korean producers to deliver fresh, globally appealing sounds while adhering to tight release schedules. The rapid turnaround often leads to reliance on pre‑existing demo libraries, which can inadvertently cause overlap with lesser‑known songs. Moreover, the incident shines a spotlight on the importance of transparent songwriting credits, especially as Western audiences increasingly scrutinize K‑Pop releases through platforms like Billboard and Spotify.

If the allegations are substantiated, YG could face legal challenges, potential royalties owed to Luna & The Sky, and a blow to Jennie’s reputation. Conversely, a cleared verdict would reaffirm the agency’s production standards and could even generate additional buzz, turning controversy into a promotional catalyst—a tactic not unfamiliar in the pop world.

## What Fans Can Expect

While the investigation unfolds, YG has hinted that “Eclipse” will still be released as scheduled on August 5, with the full music video premiering on YouTube and the track becoming available on major streaming services such as Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music. International fans can pre‑save the single on these platforms, and the music video will also be uploaded to the official BLACKPINK YouTube channel, ensuring maximum global reach.

Should the song clear all legal hurdles, Jennie is expected to promote “Eclipse” on Korean music shows like Inkigayo and M Countdown, as well as on Western talk shows that have previously featured BLACKPINK members. Additionally, YG has promised a behind‑the‑scenes documentary on the making of the track, which will likely appear on YouTube Shorts and possibly on Netflix’s “K‑Pop: The Rise” series, offering fans deeper insight into the creative process.

## 💭 Editor's Take
