The latest stills from tvN’s upcoming romance drama "My Bias, My Boss" have set fans’ hearts racing – especially after Cha Woo Min’s character, Lee Chan, takes a daring step to win the affection of Kim Hye Jun’s Nam Da Reum. The on‑screen chemistry, combined with the beloved webtoon source material, is already generating buzz about where to watch the series and how the storyline might reshape K‑Drama romance tropes.

## Background

Cha Woo Min first burst onto the K‑Pop‑turned‑acting scene as a member of the short‑lived idol group IM (I'M) before transitioning to acting with supporting roles in series like "The King: Eternal Monarch" and "All of Us Are Dead." Over the past two years, he’s built a reputation for delivering nuanced performances, earning praise for his ability to blend youthful charisma with emotional depth. Meanwhile, Kim Hye Jun, a rising actress known for her breakout role in the teen drama "School 2021," has quickly become a fan‑favorite thanks to her expressive acting and relatable on‑screen presence.

Both stars are now headlining a drama adapted from the popular webtoon "My Bias, My Boss," which amassed over 5 million reads on platforms like Naver Webtoon. The webtoon follows Nam Da Reum, a fresh graduate who joins a corporate firm hoping to encounter her long‑time idol, Lee Chan. The narrative mixes workplace comedy with heartfelt romance, a formula that has resonated with both domestic and international audiences.

The drama is slated to air on tvN, one of South Korea’s leading cable networks known for hit series such as "Crash Landing on You" and "Itaewon Class." tvN dramas often secure global distribution deals, landing on platforms like Viu, Viki, and sometimes Netflix, giving overseas fans easy access. While exact streaming partners for "My Bias, My Boss" have not been officially announced, industry insiders expect the series to appear on major streaming services within weeks of its domestic broadcast.

## What Happened

On July 22, tvN released a set of brand‑new stills that reveal a pivotal moment: Lee Chan (Cha Woo Min) is seen kneeling in the company’s rooftop garden, holding a handwritten note addressed to Nam Da Reum (Kim Hye Jun). The caption reads, “A bold move to win her heart,” confirming that the upcoming episode will feature Lee Chan’s first overt confession. This visual teaser has ignited speculation across fan forums, with many noting the symbolic use of the rooftop—a recurring motif in K‑Drama romances representing a private, elevated space away from corporate pressures.

The stills also showcase the sleek office setting, modern fashion, and the chemistry‑laden glances between the leads, hinting at a blend of corporate drama and sweet romance. In a brief statement, tvN’s marketing director emphasized that the series aims to capture “the excitement of chasing a dream while navigating real‑world challenges,” positioning the drama as both a love story and a commentary on youthful ambition.

No official trailer has dropped yet, but the promotional schedule suggests a teaser video will arrive within the next week, followed by a full trailer two weeks before the premiere. The series is expected to debut in early September, occupying tvN’s prime‑time slot on Wednesdays and Thursdays at 21:00 KST.

## Why This Matters

Cha Woo Min’s involvement marks a significant milestone in his acting career. Transitioning from idol to lead‑role actor is a path fraught with skepticism, yet his casting signals industry confidence in his talent. A successful romance drama could cement his status as a versatile star, opening doors to higher‑budget projects and potential Hollywood interest—a trend we’ve seen with other K‑Actresses and idols.

For Kim Hye Jun, “My Bias, My Boss” offers a platform to showcase her growth from supporting roles to a leading lady capable of carrying a series’ emotional weight. Her portrayal of a determined yet vulnerable newcomer resonates with a generation of Korean youth facing similar career anxieties, potentially boosting her appeal in both domestic and overseas markets.

From an industry perspective, the drama’s webtoon origins underscore the growing synergy between digital comics and television. Webtoons provide ready‑made fanbases and proven story arcs, reducing risk for broadcasters. Moreover, the romance‑workplace hybrid taps into the global appetite for feel‑good series that blend career aspirations with love, a formula that has propelled shows like "Crash Landing on You" to international fame.

## What Fans Can Expect

Viewers can anticipate a blend of light‑hearted office antics, heartfelt confessions, and stylish cinematography. The rooftop scene suggests a visually striking aesthetic, while the corporate setting promises plotlines involving promotions, rivalries, and the classic “boss‑employee” tension.

In addition to the main romance, secondary characters—played by seasoned actors such as Lee Joon‑gi (guest appearance) and newcomer Park Si‑yeon—are expected to add depth and comic relief. Fans should also watch for original soundtrack (OST) contributions from popular K‑Pop groups; tvN dramas often feature chart‑topping tracks that later dominate Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales chart.

The series will likely be available for streaming on platforms like Viu, Viki, and possibly Netflix within a week of the Korean broadcast, allowing international audiences to binge‑watch or catch up episode‑by‑episode. Keep an eye on official announcements for exact streaming dates.
