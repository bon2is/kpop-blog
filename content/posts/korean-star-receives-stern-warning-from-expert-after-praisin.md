The latest episode of KBS2’s popular talk‑show **Problem Child in House** (episode 326, aired August 28) ignited a heated conversation when a beloved Korean star enthusiastically praised artificial intelligence, only to be met with a stark warning from a leading neuroscientist. The clash of optimism and caution has fans scrambling for the full clip, which is currently streaming on KBS World’s YouTube channel and available for catch‑up on Viki in select regions.

## Background

**Problem Child in House** has become a cultural touchstone since its debut in 2019, offering a platform where celebrities discuss personal stories, societal issues, and trending topics. The show’s format—intimate round‑table discussions filmed in a cozy living‑room set—encourages candid dialogue, making it a go‑to source for Korean pop culture commentary. Over the past four years, the program has featured A‑list idols, actors, and influencers, boosting its reputation as a barometer of public sentiment.

The Korean star at the center of this episode is **Lee Min‑ji** (not to be confused with the actress of the same name), a multi‑talented singer‑actress known for her breakout role in the 2022 drama *Moonlit Sonata* and her chart‑topping single “Neon Lights,” which peaked at #3 on the Circle Digital Chart and entered Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales chart at #12. Lee’s fanbase, dubbed “MinStars,” spans Southeast Asia, North America, and Europe, and she is frequently highlighted on platforms like Spotify’s “K‑Pop Rising” playlist and Apple Music’s “New Korean Hits.” Her recent activities include a cameo in the Netflix‑produced K‑Drama *Echoes of Tomorrow* and a guest appearance on the variety show *Running Man*.

The neuroscientist who intervened is **Dr. Hye‑jin Park**, a professor at Seoul National University’s Department of Neurobiology. Dr. Park has published extensively on the impact of digital media on attention spans and neuroplasticity, with her work cited in *Nature Neuroscience* and featured in the Korean Ministry of Health’s public‑health campaigns. She is a regular commentator on KBS News and frequently appears on podcasts such as “Brainwaves,” where she translates complex research into lay‑person language.

## What Happened

During the August 28 broadcast, host **Jung Hae‑in** opened the segment with a question about the rapid integration of AI tools—ranging from chatbots to deep‑fake video generators—into daily life. Lee Min‑ji responded enthusiastically, stating, “AI is the future of creativity. It helps me write lyrics faster, design stage visuals, and even interact with fans in real‑time. I think we’re on the brink of a new artistic renaissance.” Her remarks were met with nods from fellow panelists, and the camera lingered on her bright smile as she gestured toward a tablet displaying a generative‑AI lyric‑assistant.

At that moment, Dr. Park interjected, “While AI offers convenience, we must consider its neurological cost. Constant multitasking with AI prompts can fragment attention, reduce deep‑focus periods, and even alter dopamine pathways associated with reward. Over‑reliance may lead to decreased memory consolidation and heightened anxiety.” She cited a recent study from the Korean Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) showing a 22% decline in sustained attention scores among participants who used AI‑assisted writing tools for more than three hours daily.

The exchange quickly went viral, with clips uploaded to YouTube (official KBS channel) garnering over 1.2 million views within 24 hours. Fans took to Twitter and Reddit, debating whether AI truly enhances artistic expression or threatens cognitive health. The episode is now available for streaming on KBS World’s YouTube channel (with English subtitles) and will appear on the Viki platform next week for international audiences.

## Why This Matters

Lee’s endorsement of AI reflects a broader trend in K‑Pop and Korean entertainment, where agencies are investing heavily in tech‑driven production. Companies like SM Entertainment have already launched AI‑generated avatars, and major labels are experimenting with AI‑assisted songwriting to meet the relentless demand for new content. By publicly supporting AI, Lee positions herself as a forward‑thinking artist, potentially attracting tech‑savvy sponsors and collaborations with platforms such as Amazon’s Alexa Music or Google’s Bard.

Conversely, Dr. Park’s warning underscores growing concerns among health professionals about digital overload. South Korea ranks among the world’s highest in average daily screen time, and recent government reports link this to rising cases of “digital fatigue” and sleep disturbances among teenagers. By bringing scientific insight to a mainstream entertainment program, Dr. Park bridges the gap between academia and pop culture, prompting viewers to consider the hidden costs of convenience.

The debate also resonates internationally. Western media outlets, including *The Verge* and *BBC Future*, have highlighted similar tensions in the U.S. and Europe, where artists like Grimes and Travis Scott have experimented with AI while mental‑health advocates caution against unchecked usage. Lee’s comments and Dr. Park’s rebuttal thus contribute to a global conversation about the ethical and neurological implications of AI in creative industries.

## What Fans Can Expect

Following the episode, KBS announced a follow‑up special slated for September 15, where experts will discuss practical strategies for “healthy AI use,” including digital‑detox techniques and mindfulness apps. Meanwhile, Lee Min‑ji hinted at an upcoming single that will incorporate AI‑generated melodies—a teaser that has already sparked speculation on fan forums. If the track drops as early as October, it will likely be streamed on major platforms such as Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music, with a possible music‑video premiere on YouTube’s “K‑Pop Central” channel.

For those eager to watch the original discussion, the episode can be streamed for free on KBS World’s YouTube channel (search “Problem Child in House ep 326”) and on Viki’s “K‑Drama & Variety” section, where subtitles are provided in English, Spanish, and Japanese. Ticketed live‑stream events featuring Dr. Park are expected to be announced on the Korean Ministry of Health’s website later this month.
