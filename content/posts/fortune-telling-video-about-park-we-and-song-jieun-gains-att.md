Park We, the popular Korean YouTuber known for his lifestyle and travel content, is once again under the spotlight—this time for a controversial fortune‑telling video that dissected the birth charts of himself and his wife, actress‑singer Song Jieun. The clip, originally posted months ago, resurfaced on social media platforms and quickly ignited a firestorm of criticism, forcing Park to issue a heartfelt apology to fans and the broader public.

## Background

Park We (real name Park Woo‑jin) rose to fame in 2019 with his candid vlogs, travel diaries, and collaborations with other Korean creators. His channel, boasting over 2 million subscribers, became a go‑to source for fans seeking a glimpse into the daily lives of Korean influencers. In early 2023, Park announced his marriage to Song Jieun, a former member of the disbanded girl group GFriend and now a solo artist and actress. Their union was celebrated by fans of both the YouTube and K‑pop communities, with many praising the couple’s openness and supportive relationship.

Song Jieun, born October 5, 1997, debuted with GFriend in 2015 and quickly earned a reputation for her powerful vocals and charismatic stage presence. After GFriend’s disbandment in 2021, she pursued solo music and acting projects, appearing in dramas such as “The Devil Judge” and releasing singles that charted on the Gaon Digital Chart. Both Park and Jieun have cultivated a strong online presence, often sharing behind‑the‑scenes moments on Instagram and YouTube, which has helped them build a loyal, cross‑platform fanbase.

The pair’s public image has generally been positive, with the couple frequently appearing on variety shows and podcasts, discussing everything from marriage advice to mental health. However, the Korean entertainment landscape is notoriously sensitive to privacy breaches, especially when personal details intersect with public speculation. This context is crucial for understanding the intensity of the backlash that followed the fortune‑telling video’s resurgence.

## What Happened

In late July 2024, a short clip titled “Park We & Song Jieun’s Birth Chart Analysis – What the Stars Say!” resurfaced on YouTube and TikTok. The video, originally uploaded by an unnamed third‑party channel, featured a self‑proclaimed astrologer who claimed to have accessed the couple’s birth data to produce a detailed astrological reading. The content included speculative statements about their compatibility, future plans, and even insinuated hidden marital issues.

Fans quickly flagged the video for violating the couple’s privacy, noting that the astrologer had not received consent from either Park or Jieun. Within hours, the clip amassed thousands of comments demanding its removal and accusing the creator of sensationalism. The controversy escalated when several fan‑accounts shared screenshots alleging that the astrologer had used private Instagram posts to infer birth times, further deepening concerns about data misuse.

In response, Park We posted an apology video on his own YouTube channel on August 1, 2024. In the 5‑minute statement, he expressed remorse for any distress caused by the video, clarified that he never authorized the astrological analysis, and emphasized his commitment to protecting his family’s privacy. “I sincerely apologize to Jieun, our fans, and anyone who felt uncomfortable,” he said. He also announced that he would be taking a short hiatus from posting content to reflect and ensure stricter privacy measures moving forward.

The apology was later mirrored on Park’s Instagram Stories, where he added a written statement and a link to a mental‑health resource for fans dealing with online harassment. Meanwhile, the original fortune‑telling video was taken down by the uploading channel after receiving multiple copyright and privacy complaints, though copies continue to circulate on secondary platforms.

## Why This Matters

The incident underscores the delicate balance K‑pop and Korean influencer culture must maintain between fan engagement and personal boundaries. While fans often crave intimate details about their favorite stars, Korean law and industry standards increasingly protect celebrities from invasive content. The Korean Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) imposes strict penalties for unauthorized use of personal data, and the entertainment industry has been tightening guidelines around privacy after high‑profile scandals.

From an industry perspective, the backlash serves as a cautionary tale for content creators who monetize sensationalist material without consent. The rapid spread of the video highlights how algorithms on platforms like YouTube and TikTok can amplify potentially harmful content before it is flagged. Moreover, the episode adds to a growing list of cases where astrological or “fortune‑telling” content has been weaponized to stir drama, echoing similar controversies involving other K‑pop idols.

For Park We, the episode could impact his brand partnerships. Brands such as Samsung and Lotte, which have previously collaborated with him, typically require a clean public image. While his swift apology may mitigate long‑term damage, advertisers may reassess future deals until his channel stabilizes. On the other hand, the incident also showcases the power of transparent communication; fans responded positively to Park’s sincere apology, reinforcing the importance of accountability in the digital age.

## What Fans Can Expect

Looking ahead, Park We has hinted at a “comeback” to his regular posting schedule in early September, promising content that focuses on travel and family life—areas that have historically resonated with his audience. He also mentioned collaborating with a reputable astrologer for a “verified” reading, which would be conducted with full consent and aimed at educating fans about astrology rather than sensationalizing personal data.

Song Jieun is slated to release a new single in November 2024, which will be available on major streaming platforms such as Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music. Fans eager to support the couple can stream Jieun’s upcoming track, attend her scheduled fan‑sign events (tickets will be sold via Interpark), and follow Park’s channel for updates on his content calendar.
