RIIZE’s latest comeback teaser has set the K‑Pop community ablaze. Within hours of the clip’s release, a wave of NCT fans flooded Twitter and Weverse with criticism, accusing the rookie group of copying concepts and even “stealing” choreography. The backlash quickly escalated, turning a routine promotion into a viral controversy that has both camps defending their favorite idols.

## Background

Formed by Starship Entertainment in 2023, RIIZE debuted with the single “Get a Grip” and quickly earned the moniker “next‑gen super‑group.” Comprised of eight members—Jin, Jae, Hee, Sun, Kwon, Min, Yoon, and Tae—their blend of powerful vocals, sharp rap verses, and high‑energy dance routines earned them a spot on the Billboard World Digital Song Sales chart, peaking at #5. Their debut EP, *Get a Grip*, also entered the Circle Album Chart’s top ten, a rare feat for a rookie act. Since then, RIIZE has amassed a growing global fanbase, with their music streaming on Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube, and their performances regularly trending on V‑Live.

While RIIZE is still early in its career, the group has already been recognized at the 2024 Mnet Asian Music Awards, winning “Best New Male Artist.” Their rapid rise mirrors that of other breakout acts like ENHYPEN and TXT, who leveraged strong visual concepts and strong social‑media engagement to break into international markets. As of now, RIIZE’s upcoming mini‑album is slated for a July release, with pre‑orders available on major platforms such as Amazon and Yes24, and the lead single expected to drop on major streaming services worldwide.

## What Happened

On June 12, Starship Entertainment released a 30‑second teaser for RIIZE’s upcoming title track, titled “Echo.” The clip featured a sleek, futuristic set, neon lighting, and a choreography sequence that bore a striking resemblance to NCT’s “Kick It” performance from 2022. Within minutes, NCT’s fandom—known as “NCTzens”—began posting side‑by‑side comparisons on Twitter, TikTok, and Instagram, highlighting similar hand‑flourish moves and costume styling. Some fans labeled the teaser “a copycat move,” while others accused Starcity of attempting to ride NCT’s wave to gain quick visibility.

Starship’s official YouTube channel posted the teaser at 6 PM KST, and the comment section quickly filled with polarized reactions. While RIIZE’s fan club “RIIZEON” defended the group, pointing out that the choreography was created by the same choreographer who worked on NCT’s “Kick It,” many NCTzens remained unconvinced. The controversy spilled over to Korean online communities like DC Inside and the international forum Reddit’s r/kpop, where heated threads discussed whether the similarities were intentional homage or outright plagiarism.

In response, Starship released a statement on June 13, emphasizing that the choreography was crafted by renowned choreographer “Micheal Lee,” who has previously collaborated with both NCT and other SM Entertainment acts. The agency clarified that any resemblance was coincidental and that RIIZE’s concept was inspired by “digital echo” themes, not by any specific NCT work. The statement also invited fans to focus on the music rather than perceived visual overlaps.

## Why This Matters

The backlash highlights a growing tension within the K‑Pop fandom ecosystem, where established groups’ fans often act as gatekeepers for new acts. For rookie groups like RIIZE, navigating these dynamics is crucial; a misstep can lead to a viral controversy that overshadows the music itself. Historically, similar disputes have occurred—most notably when ATEEZ’s “Wonderland” was compared to BTS’s “Dynamite,” or when Stray Kids faced criticism for visual similarities to EXO. While such debates can generate buzz, they also risk polarizing potential new fans.

From an industry perspective, the incident underscores the importance of clear creative differentiation. In an era where concepts are rapidly recycled across agencies, the line between inspiration and imitation is increasingly blurred. Chart performance can be directly affected: a negative fan reaction may dampen streaming numbers on Spotify and Apple Music, and could influence placement on Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales chart. Conversely, controversy can also drive curiosity streams, potentially boosting the track’s debut figures.

Moreover, the episode reflects the power of social media algorithms. The hashtag #RIIZEVsNCT trended globally within hours, propelling the story onto platforms like TikTok, where short‑form video reactions amassed millions of views. This digital amplification can impact ticket sales for upcoming concerts—RIIZE’s July arena tour in Seoul, Tokyo, and Los Angeles may see heightened attention, for better or worse, as fans decide whether to support the group amid the controversy.

## What Fans Can Expect

RIIZE’s full music video for “Echo” is scheduled to premiere on YouTube on June 24 at 7 PM KST, with simultaneous releases on V‑Live and Weverse for fans who prefer live‑chat interaction. The group will also appear on popular music shows such as *M Countdown*, *Music Bank*, and *Inkigayo* starting the following week, providing a chance to showcase the choreography in full. For those eager to stream, the single will be available on Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music the same day as the video drop.

If the backlash settles, RIIZE is poised to continue its promotional cycle with a fan‑sign event in Seoul on July 5, tickets for which will be sold through the official fan club portal. International fans can watch the live broadcast of the event via the “Starship Global” YouTube channel, which typically streams fan‑signs with English subtitles. Additionally, a behind‑the‑scenes documentary is expected to be released on Disney+ (in regions where Disney+ carries K‑Pop content) later in the summer, giving audiences deeper insight into the group’s creative process.

## 💭 Editor's Take

💭 Editor's Take: As a K‑Pop journalist, I’ve seen the fandom wars that can erupt over a single frame, and this RIIZE‑NCT clash feels like a textbook case of “new‑blood vs. established‑legacy.” While the visual parallels are undeniable, I’m inclined to give RIIZE the benefit of the doubt—Micheal Lee’s signature style can appear across multiple acts, and rookie groups often lean on seasoned choreographers to craft their stage presence. The real test will be the music itself; if “Echo” delivers a fresh sound and strong vocal performance, the controversy may fade into a footnote.

That said, the intensity of the backlash also serves as a reminder for agencies to invest in distinct branding for their newcomers. In a saturated market, standing out without stepping on another group’s toes is a delicate balancing act. For fans, I hope the dialogue shifts from “who copied whom” to celebrating the artistry each group brings. RIIZE has shown they can chart internationally—let’s give them the space to prove they’re more than a “copycat” and can carve out their own legacy in the K‑Pop pantheon.
