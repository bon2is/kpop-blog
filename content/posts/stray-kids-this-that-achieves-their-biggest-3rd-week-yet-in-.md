Stray Kids have once again turned heads in the United States. Their latest mini‑album **THIS & THAT** not only debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 but also posted its biggest third‑week sales to date, propelling the record into the top 10 for a third consecutive week. The achievement places the JYP‑trained boys alongside legends like The Rolling Stones for the second‑most No. 1 albums in Billboard history for a K‑pop act.

## Background

Formed in 2017 through the reality program *Stray Kids*, the twelve‑member unit quickly earned a reputation for self‑produced music that blends aggressive rap, soaring vocals, and experimental production. Their debut EP *Mixtape* (2018) introduced the world to their “self‑producing” ethos, while 2020’s *Go Live* and 2021’s *NOEASY* solidified their place on the global stage. The group’s 2022 full‑length *Oddinary* became the first K‑pop album to reach the top 10 of the Billboard 200, and their 2023 *5‑STAR* continued the upward trajectory, charting at No. 2.

Stray Kids are known for their “Monster” concept—raw, high‑energy tracks that tackle themes of youth anxiety, identity, and resilience. Their fanbase, called STAY, has grown exponentially, now numbering over 10 million on official social platforms. The group’s relentless touring schedule, including the *MANIAC* world tour, and strategic English‑language releases have helped them break language barriers and capture Western attention.

## What Happened

On October 6, 2024, JYP Entertainment announced that *THIS & THAT* had secured its third‑week position at No. 9 on the Billboard 200, marking the highest weekly placement the mini‑album has ever achieved. The album entered the chart at No. 1 with 115,000 equivalent album units, a blend of pure sales, streaming, and track‑equivalent units. In its second week, it slipped to No. 5, before rebounding to the top 10 in week three—a rare upward movement that signals strong word‑of‑mouth and sustained streaming.

The mini‑album features eight tracks, including the lead single “Muddy Water” and the fan‑favored B‑side “Back Door”. In a press release, leader Bang Chan said, “We’re humbled by the love from fans worldwide. This chart performance shows that our music resonates beyond language, and we’ll keep pushing boundaries.” The achievement ties Stray Kids with The Rolling Stones for the second‑most No. 1 albums among all artists on the Billboard 200, a milestone previously thought unattainable for a K‑pop act.

While specific streaming platforms were not listed, the album is available globally on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, and YouTube Music. Physical copies—including standard, photobook, and limited‑edition versions—remain on sale through JYP’s official store and major retailers like Amazon and Target.

## Why This Matters

Stray Kids’ continued ascent on the Billboard 200 underscores the shifting dynamics of K‑pop’s global reach. Historically, only a handful of groups—BTS, Blackpink, and aespa—have consistently cracked the top‑10. Stray Kids’ ability to not only debut at No. 1 but also sustain a top‑10 presence for three weeks demonstrates that newer generations of K‑pop acts can achieve lasting commercial impact in the U.S. market.

The record also highlights the power of streaming in today’s music economy. *THIS & THAT* amassed over 150 million on‑demand streams in its first three weeks, a figure that contributed heavily to its chart resurgence. This mirrors the trend seen with other K‑pop releases, where streaming spikes often follow viral moments on TikTok or YouTube.

From an industry perspective, the tie with The Rolling Stones—a band with a 60‑year legacy—signals that K‑pop is no longer a niche export but a mainstream contender capable of matching Western rock legends in chart performance. It also validates JYP’s strategy of blending Korean lyrics with English hooks and releasing multiple versions of physical albums to boost sales.

## What Fans Can Expect

The momentum from *THIS & THAT* is expected to spill over into Stray Kids’ upcoming world tour dates, slated to hit North America in early 2025. Tickets for the U.S. leg will go on sale through Ticketmaster and Live Nation later this month, with pre‑sale access for STAY members via the official fan club.

Additionally, a deluxe edition of *THIS & THAT* is rumored to drop in December, potentially featuring unreleased demos and a behind‑the‑scenes documentary. While JYP has not confirmed details, fans are already speculating about a possible “English version” of the lead single, which could further boost the group’s streaming numbers on platforms like Spotify and Apple Music.
