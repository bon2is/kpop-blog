Chaeryeong, the charismatic lead dancer of JYP’s powerhouse girl group ITZY, is gearing up for a solo debut this November. The announcement, confirmed by both JTBC Entertainment News and JYP Entertainment, has already set social media ablaze, as fans worldwide scramble to find out where they can stream the upcoming track and watch the music video.

## Background

Formed in 2019, ITZY quickly rose to prominence with their bold “girl crush” concept and empowering anthems like “DALLA DALLA,” “WANNABE,” and “LOCO.” The six‑member unit—Yeji, Lia, Ryujin, Chaeryeong, Yuna, and Leeseo—has amassed a dedicated global fanbase, known as MIDZY, and consistently lands high on both South Korea’s Circle Chart and the Billboard World Digital Song Sales chart. Their discography, characterized by fierce choreography and confident lyrics, has earned them multiple Daesang awards and a spot on the 2022 Billboard Year‑End Top K‑Pop Artists list.

Chaeryeong, born on April 5, 2001, first entered the public eye as a contestant on the survival show "K‑Pop Star Hunt" before joining JYP’s trainee program. Within ITZY, she is celebrated for her technical dance prowess, subtle vocal tone, and charismatic stage presence. While the group’s releases have showcased her abilities, Chaeryeong has also contributed to songwriting, co‑writing the rap sections for “Not Shy” and “Mafia In the Morning.” Her solo venture marks the first time the member will step out of the group’s collective brand to explore a personal musical identity.

In recent months, ITZY has been busy promoting their EP "CHERRY," which dropped in February 2024 and topped the Circle Album Chart for two consecutive weeks. The group’s world tour, “CHECKMATE,” is currently touring North America and Europe, with tickets selling out in major cities like Los Angeles, London, and Seoul. Amid this momentum, Chaeryeong’s solo project offers a fresh focal point for both the group’s and JYP’s 2024‑2025 promotional calendar.

## What Happened

On August 27, JTBC Entertainment News broke the story that Chaeryeong would release solo music in November, adding that a music video shoot was already underway. JYP Entertainment swiftly issued an official statement confirming the news: “It is true that Chaeryeong will be making her solo debut. She is preparing a new song and music video, and we look forward to sharing more details soon.” No official title or concept images have been released yet, but insiders hint that the track will blend contemporary R&B with synth‑pop elements, showcasing Chaeryeong’s smooth vocal timbre and dance‑centric performance style.

While exact release dates remain under wraps, JYP has indicated that the single will be available across all major streaming platforms—including Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music—on the same day worldwide. The accompanying music video is expected to premiere on YouTube and possibly be featured on JYP’s official V‑Live channel for a limited‑time live watch party. Additionally, the label plans to push the track to Korean music shows such as "Music Bank" and "Inkigayo," giving Chaeryeong a chance to earn her first solo music‑show win.

Industry insiders also note that Chaeryeong’s solo debut could be part of JYP’s broader strategy to diversify its roster’s individual activities, similar to previous solo releases from group members like TWICE’s Nayeon and Stray Kids’ Hyunjin. The timing aligns with the group’s mid‑tour break, allowing Chaeryeong to focus on recording and promotion without conflicting with ITZY’s group schedule.

## Why This Matters

Chaeryeong’s solo debut is significant on several fronts. First, it underscores JYP Entertainment’s confidence in the individual marketability of its artists. By investing in a solo project for a member who has traditionally been seen as the “dance ace,” JYP signals a shift toward highlighting vocal and artistic versatility within its groups. This could inspire other agencies to explore similar pathways for their idols, potentially diversifying the K‑pop soundscape.

Second, the release arrives at a moment when solo projects from girl‑group members are gaining unprecedented chart traction in the West. For instance, BLACKPINK’s Lisa topped the Billboard Hot 100 with "Lalisa," and TWICE’s Nayeon entered the Billboard 200’s top ten with her debut EP. If Chaeryeong’s track lands on the Billboard World Digital Song Sales chart or even cracks the Hot 100, it would reinforce the growing power of K‑pop solo acts in the global market.

Third, the anticipation surrounding the music video’s visual concept could set new standards for production value. ITZY’s past videos are known for vibrant colors and kinetic choreography; Chaeryeong’s solo MV may push the envelope further, possibly incorporating cinematic storytelling or cutting‑edge CGI. Such a high‑budget rollout would not only elevate her personal brand but also contribute to the overall reputation of Korean music videos on platforms like YouTube, where K‑pop content consistently dominates view counts.

## What Fans Can Expect

Fans can look forward to a multi‑phase promotional schedule. In the weeks leading up to the November release, JYP is likely to drop teaser images, concept photos, and short video snippets on the group’s official Instagram and Twitter accounts. A pre‑release single or lyric video could also be part of the strategy, designed to build streaming momentum before the full drop.

On the day of release, the song will be accessible on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, and possibly exclusive platforms like Melon and Genie for the Korean market. The music video will debut on YouTube, with a simultaneous premiere on V‑Live for fans who prefer a live‑chat experience. Expect a series of dance practice videos, behind‑the‑scenes clips, and a possible “making‑of” documentary to be released over the following weeks, keeping the conversation alive on social media.

Finally, Chaeryeong may appear on variety shows such as "Running Man" or "Weekly Idol" to promote the solo effort, giving international fans additional opportunities to see her personality off‑stage. These appearances often drive spikes in streaming numbers, as seen with other solo debuts.
