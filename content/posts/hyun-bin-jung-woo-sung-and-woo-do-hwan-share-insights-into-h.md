Hyun Bin, Jung Woo‑sung and Woo Do‑hwan sit down with ELLE Korea to unpack the dramatic transformation of their characters in Made in Korea 2. The actors reveal how ambition, morality and personal sacrifice have been re‑imagined for a sequel that promises higher stakes and deeper emotional stakes.

## Background

Made in Korea debuted in 2022 as a gritty, high‑concept thriller that blended corporate intrigue with legal drama. Starring Hyun Bin as Baek Ki‑tae, a ruthless mogul obsessed with wealth, and Jung Woo‑sung as prosecutor Jang Geon‑young, a principled lawyer willing to risk everything to bring Ki‑tae down, the series quickly became a talk‑show staple in South Korea and attracted a growing international fanbase. The first season’s success was reflected in strong viewership numbers on Disney+, where it premiered globally, and it earned a spot on the weekly Circle Top 10 streaming chart.

Hyun Bin, already a household name thanks to hits like “Crash Landing on You” and “Secret Love Affair,” leveraged his star power to elevate the series, while Jung Woo‑sung—renowned for his intense performances in “The King” and “A Taxi Driver”—brought a gravitas that anchored the legal battle. Woo Do‑hwan, a rising actor known for supporting roles in “Vincenzo” and “Start-Up,” joined the original cast as a new ally whose loyalties remain ambiguous. Together, they created a dynamic that resonated with both domestic and overseas audiences, leading to the drama’s inclusion on several “Best K‑Drama of 2022” lists, including Billboard’s K‑Pop & K‑Drama roundup.

The first season’s narrative arc concluded with Ki‑tae’s empire teetering on collapse and Geon‑young walking away from the justice system, leaving fans hungry for answers. Anticipation built around a sequel that would explore the aftermath of those choices, and the production team confirmed that Made in Korea 2 would return with a fresh storyline while retaining the core thematic tension of power versus conscience.

## What Happened

In a recent ELLE Korea pictorial, Hyun Bin, Jung Woo‑sung and Woo Do‑hwan shared exclusive insights into how their characters have evolved for the second season. Hyun Bin explained that Baek Ki‑tae is no longer the one‑dimensional villain of season one; instead, he is now a man forced to confront the human cost of his ambition after a personal tragedy shakes his empire. “I’m playing a Ki‑tae who finally feels the weight of his actions,” Bin said. “He’s still ambitious, but there’s a vulnerability that wasn’t there before.”

Jung Woo‑sung described Jang Geon‑young’s shift from a relentless prosecutor to a more nuanced figure wrestling with disillusionment. After abandoning the courtroom, Geon‑young now works as a private investigator, using his legal knowledge to expose corruption from the shadows. “It’s a different kind of fight,” Woo‑sung noted. “He’s still fighting for justice, but the battlefield has moved.”

Woo Do‑hwan’s character, originally introduced as a corporate fixer, is now positioned as a potential bridge between Ki‑tae and Geon‑young. He reveals that his loyalties are tested when a new corporate scandal threatens to engulf both former foes. “My role is to keep the audience guessing,” he said, hinting at possible alliances that could reshape the power dynamics.

The trio also confirmed that Made in Korea 2 will premiere on Disney+ worldwide in early 2025, with subtitles in multiple languages. While exact dates remain unannounced, Disney+ has promised a “global release” that will make the series accessible to the same international audience that propelled the first season to success.

## Why This Matters

The evolution of the main characters signals a broader trend in K‑drama storytelling: moving beyond clear‑cut heroes and villains toward morally complex protagonists. This shift mirrors the success of series like “Squid Game” and “All of Us Are Dead,” which captivated global viewers with layered narratives. By deepening Ki‑tae’s humanity and expanding Geon‑young’s investigative role, Made in Korea 2 positions itself to compete for both critical acclaim and streaming records.

From an industry standpoint, the sequel reinforces Disney+’s commitment to high‑budget Korean content, a strategy that has paid off with titles such as “Hometown Cha‑Cha‑Cha” and “The Glory.” The platform’s investment in original Korean productions is part of a larger arms race among streaming giants—Netflix, Amazon Prime Video and Apple TV+—to capture the lucrative K‑drama market. A strong performance by Made in Korea 2 could solidify Disney+ as the go‑to service for premium Korean thrillers.

Chart-wise, the first season’s strong performance on the Circle Top 10 and its placement on Billboard’s “World Albums” chart (as a soundtrack release) suggest that the sequel could replicate or surpass those numbers. If the series maintains its momentum, we may see it break into the top 5 of the weekly Global Streaming Chart on Disney+, a milestone that would further validate the global appetite for Korean legal‑thriller narratives.

## What Fans Can Expect

Fans can look forward to a darker, more introspective tone, with flashbacks that reveal Ki‑tae’s upbringing and Geon‑young’s personal losses. The production team promises “cinematic” visuals, with on‑location shoots in Seoul’s financial district and international settings that hint at a possible expansion of the story’s scope beyond Korea.

The sequel will also introduce new supporting characters, including a whistleblower played by a popular K‑pop idol turned actor, which is expected to boost cross‑media buzz. Additionally, the original soundtrack will feature contributions from acclaimed Korean musicians, and the lead single is slated for release on Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube Music a week before the series drops.

## 💭 Editor's Take

💭 Editor's Take: Made in Korea 2 feels like a bold gamble that could pay off in spades. Hyun Bin’s willingness to expose a softer side of his notorious mogul shows a maturity that fans have been craving, while Jung Woo‑sung’s pivot to a shadowy investigator keeps his character fresh and unpredictable. The decision to keep the series on Disney+ ensures a seamless viewing experience for the global audience that fell in love with the first season’s razor‑sharp tension. As someone who binge‑watched the original, I’m thrilled to see the narrative stakes raised and can’t wait to see how the new moral gray zones play out on screen. If the chemistry remains as electric as before, Made in Korea 2 could become the next benchmark for Korean thriller dramas on the streaming stage.
