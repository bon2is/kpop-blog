Zhoumi, the charismatic Chinese member of SUPER JUNIOR‑M, has just been appointed CEO of a brand‑new joint venture between SM Entertainment and Tencent Music Entertainment Group (TME). Announced on August 27, the venture—named STE—will operate out of Beijing and is poised to reshape how K‑Pop content is distributed across China’s massive streaming ecosystem.

## Background

SUPER JUNIOR‑M debuted in 2020 as a sub‑unit of the legendary boy group SUPER JUNIOR, focusing on the Chinese market with Mandarin releases and a distinct musical style. The unit consists of three members—Zhoumi, Eunhyuk, and Donghae—who have already delivered hits like “Swing” and the Chinese‑language single “Super Clap.” Zhoumi, born Li Zhuo‑ming in 1991, first entered the K‑Pop scene as a trainee at SM Entertainment in 2009 and quickly became known for his smooth vocals, fluency in both Mandarin and Korean, and his work as a solo artist on tracks such as “Just One Day” and “Better Together.”

Beyond his musical achievements, Zhoumi has built a reputation as a cultural bridge between South Korea and China. He has hosted Chinese variety shows, acted in dramas, and served as an ambassador for SM’s Chinese promotions. His deep understanding of both markets makes him a logical choice for a leadership role that aims to fuse K‑Pop production power with China’s streaming dominance.

SM Entertainment, founded in 1995, is one of the “Big Three” Korean agencies and has long sought to expand its footprint in Mainland China. Previous attempts—including the establishment of SM Shanghai and partnerships with local labels—have met with mixed success due to regulatory hurdles and shifting market dynamics. Tencent Music Entertainment Group, the operator of China’s top music platforms QQ Music, Kugou, and Kuwo, commands over 800 million monthly active users, making it the ideal distribution partner for any K‑Pop content aiming for Chinese audiences.

## What Happened

On August 27, SM Entertainment released an official statement confirming the creation of STE (SM‑Tencent Entertainment), a joint venture headquartered in Beijing. The statement highlighted STE’s mission: “to develop, produce, and distribute high‑quality music and related content that resonates with Chinese listeners while leveraging SM’s global brand and Tencent’s unrivaled streaming infrastructure.” Zhoumi was named CEO, tasked with overseeing content strategy, artist development, and cross‑border collaborations.

In a brief interview with Soompi, Zhoumi expressed his excitement: “I’ve always dreamed of bridging the two cultures I love. As CEO, I hope to bring fresh Korean music to Chinese fans and also showcase Chinese talent on a global stage.” SM’s CEO Lee Sung‑soo added, “Zhoumi’s unique background and industry insight make him the perfect leader for this venture. STE will be a catalyst for innovative music experiences that benefit both our artists and Tencent’s users.”

While specific project details remain under wraps, the joint venture is expected to roll out exclusive releases, live‑streamed concerts, and fan‑interaction events on Tencent’s platforms. Industry insiders suggest that STE may also explore AI‑driven content curation and virtual‑reality concerts, leveraging Tencent’s tech capabilities.

## Why This Matters

Zhoumi’s appointment is a watershed moment for several reasons. First, it signals SM Entertainment’s renewed commitment to the Chinese market after a period of regulatory uncertainty that saw many Korean agencies pull back in 2020‑2021. By placing a Chinese‑born executive at the helm, SM demonstrates cultural sensitivity and a strategic pivot toward localized leadership.

Second, the partnership taps into Tencent Music’s unrivaled reach. According to a 2023 report, QQ Music alone accounts for over 30% of China’s digital music revenue. STE’s ability to place K‑Pop releases directly onto these platforms could dramatically boost streaming numbers, chart performance on China’s Billboard China Top 100, and even influence global charts through aggregated data.

Third, the venture may set a precedent for other Korean agencies seeking similar joint ventures. If STE succeeds in delivering chart‑topping releases and profitable fan‑engagement models, agencies like YG, JYP, and Big Hit could follow suit, further integrating the Asian music ecosystem.

Finally, for fans, Zhoumi’s leadership promises more tailored content. Historically, Chinese fans have faced delayed releases, limited access to official merchandise, and occasional censorship. STE’s Beijing base could streamline licensing, provide simultaneous releases, and ensure that fan‑generated content complies with local regulations while retaining artistic integrity.

## What Fans Can Expect

In the coming months, STE is expected to unveil a slate of projects. Rumors suggest a collaborative single featuring Zhoumi, a Chinese‑language track by a SM‑signed artist, and a virtual concert series streamed exclusively on QQ Music and WeChat Live. Additionally, STE may launch a “Super Junior‑M China Tour” with Zhoumi as a co‑producer, offering fans behind‑the‑scenes access via Tencent’s fan‑club apps.

International fans should also keep an eye on streaming platforms like Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube, where STE‑produced songs will likely be released simultaneously worldwide. While the primary focus is China, the joint venture’s global distribution strategy means that non‑Chinese listeners will still have access, often with subtitles and localized marketing.
