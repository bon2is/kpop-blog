IVE’s An Yu Jin will still be on stage for the group’s Hong Kong concert, but she will sit out the high‑energy dance routines to protect a lingering knee injury. The decision, announced by Starship Entertainment on September 4, has fans rallying around the singer while also raising questions about the group’s upcoming promotions.

## Background

Formed by Starship Entertainment in December 2021, IVE quickly rose to become one of the most dominant fourth‑generation girl groups. The five‑member unit—Wonyoung, Yujin, Rei, Leeseo, and Gaeul—debuted with the single “Elephant,” but it was the follow‑up “Love Dive” that catapulted them onto the global stage, earning a perfect all‑kill on Korean charts and topping the Billboard World Digital Song Sales chart.

Since then, IVE has amassed a string of chart‑topping releases: the EP *After Like* (2022) broke streaming records on Spotify and Apple Music, while the single “I’m In Love” (2023) secured a spot on the Billboard Hot 100, a rare feat for a K‑pop girl group. Their rapid ascent is reflected in Circle Album Chart sales—*After Like* sold over 1 million copies in its first week, earning a ‘Million’ certification. Internationally, the group has been featured on Netflix’s *K‑Pop Evolution* documentary and performed on *The Late Show with Stephen Colbert*, solidifying their status as a global act.

An Yu Jin, born March 31 2003, serves as one of IVE’s main vocalists and a visual presence. Though she debuted at a young age, Yu Jin has already contributed to the group’s songwriting credits and is praised for her clear tone and stage charisma. Her recent involvement in variety shows such as *Running Man* and *Weekly Idol* has expanded her individual fanbase, making her a key figure in the group’s promotional activities.

## What Happened

On September 4, Starship Entertainment released an official statement confirming that An Yu Jin will attend the upcoming Hong Kong concert scheduled for early October but will not participate in the choreography for any of the performance segments. The agency cited “the need to prevent further aggravation of her knee injury” and emphasized that the decision was made in consultation with medical professionals. The statement read: “Yu‑Jin’s health and well‑being remain our top priority. She will join the concert in a vocal capacity and engage with fans during the event.”

The knee issue reportedly stems from a minor sprain sustained during rehearsals for the *Love Dive* world tour last month. While the injury is not deemed severe enough to halt her overall activities, the demanding choreography—particularly the high‑impact jumps in songs like “After Love” and “I’m In Love”—could risk long‑term damage if she continues to perform full dance routines.

Fans were first alerted to the situation via Starship’s official Twitter account, where the agency posted a short video clip of Yu Jin speaking directly to fans, expressing gratitude for their support and promising to give her “all the love” through her vocal performance. The post quickly trended on Korean Twitter, with the hashtag #YuJinKnee trending for several hours. International fans took to platforms such as Reddit’s r/kpop and Twitter, sharing well‑wishes and creating fan‑made “Get Well Soon” video edits.

## Why This Matters

The decision to limit Yu Jin’s dance participation underscores a growing industry shift toward prioritizing idol health over relentless performance schedules. In recent years, high‑profile cases—such as BTS’s Jin undergoing surgery for a shoulder injury and EXO’s Kai taking a hiatus for mental health—have prompted agencies to adopt more transparent health policies. Starship’s proactive announcement reflects an acknowledgment that fans value artist well‑being as much as flawless performances.

From a commercial standpoint, IVE’s upcoming Hong Kong concert is part of a broader Asian tour that includes stops in Seoul, Bangkok, and Manila. The tour is expected to boost album sales for *After Like* and generate significant streaming spikes on platforms like Spotify and Apple Music. By keeping Yu Jin on stage, even in a limited capacity, Starship ensures the group retains its full vocal harmony, preserving the concert’s musical integrity while avoiding a potential backlash that could arise from a full member’s absence.

Moreover, the incident highlights the physical toll of K‑pop choreography. Songs in IVE’s catalog often feature fast‑paced, intricate dance breaks that demand high stamina. The industry’s rigorous rehearsal schedules—sometimes exceeding 12 hours a day—have been linked to repetitive strain injuries. Yu Jin’s situation may prompt other agencies to reassess rehearsal intensity, especially for groups with demanding performance concepts.

## What Fans Can Expect

Fans attending the Hong Kong concert can look forward to a full vocal set from Yu Jin, with the group likely arranging stage positions that minimize movement for her while still showcasing her voice. The setlist is expected to include fan‑favorites like “Love Dive,” “After Like,” and the newly released track “I’m In Love,” all of which feature strong vocal sections that Yu Jin can dominate.

In addition, Starship hinted that a special “solo stage” may be added for Yu Jin, allowing her to perform an acoustic version of a recent ballad—potentially a new B‑side from the *After Like* era. This would give fans a unique live experience and compensate for the reduced dance involvement. The agency also promised exclusive behind‑the‑scenes footage on IVE’s official YouTube channel after the concert, giving fans a glimpse of the rehearsal adjustments made for Yu Jin’s health.

Looking ahead, the group is slated to release a summer digital single in November, and Yu Jin’s recovery timeline suggests she will resume full choreography by early 2025, assuming a smooth rehabilitation process. Fans should stay tuned to Starship’s official channels for updates on her progress and any potential changes to the tour schedule.
