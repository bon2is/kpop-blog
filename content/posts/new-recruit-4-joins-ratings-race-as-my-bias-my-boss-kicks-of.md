The highly‑anticipated fourth season of ENA’s black‑comedy military drama **New Recruit** exploded onto screens this week, pulling an impressive 2.8% average nationwide viewership for its premiere episode, according to Nielsen Korea. The strong start thrust the series into a heated ratings race just as the second half of **My Bias, My Boss** began its own broadcast run, setting the stage for an exciting showdown among Korean drama fans.

## Background

**New Recruit** first debuted in 2022, quickly becoming a cultural phenomenon for its razor‑sharp satire of South Korea’s mandatory military service. The series follows a group of fresh conscripts navigating the absurdities of army life while confronting broader societal issues such as class disparity, gender expectations, and bureaucratic red tape. Its unique blend of dark humor, heartfelt moments, and a stellar ensemble cast—including rising stars Lee Jae‑won, Park Seo‑yeon, and veteran actor Kim Min‑seok—earned it both critical acclaim and a devoted fanbase.

The drama’s first three seasons consistently topped weekly cable ratings, often surpassing 3% and securing spots on Netflix’s “Top 10 Korean TV Shows” list. Internationally, **New Recruit** gained traction on streaming platforms like Viki and Kocowa, where subtitles in multiple languages helped it reach audiences across the U.S., U.K., Canada, and Australia. The series also sparked lively discussions on social media, with hashtags such as #NewRecruit and #MilitaryDrama trending during each broadcast week.

After a year‑long hiatus, ENA announced the return of **New Recruit** with a fresh storyline titled “New Recruit 4,” promising deeper character development and a new set of comedic dilemmas. The production team hinted at cameo appearances from popular K‑pop idols and a tighter focus on the psychological toll of military service, raising expectations among both domestic and global viewers.

## What Happened

The premiere episode aired on **April 15, 2026**, on ENA at 21:00 KST, and immediately drew a **2.8% average nationwide rating**, according to Nielsen Korea’s latest report. This figure places the drama among the top‑performing cable shows for the week, narrowly edging out the competing broadcast of **My Bias, My Boss** (Season 2), which recorded a 2.6% rating in its opening episode.

During the episode, the new protagonist, Sergeant‑in‑training **Jung Hae‑jin** (played by Lee Jae‑won), discovers a hidden stash of vintage mixtapes in the barracks, leading to a hilarious yet poignant flashback sequence that explores the evolution of K‑pop within the military. The episode also featured a surprise cameo by idol **IU**, who performed an acoustic rendition of her 2015 hit “Palette,” marking the first time a mainstream K‑pop star has appeared in the series.

ENA released a short press statement highlighting the drama’s “bold narrative direction” and “commitment to authentic storytelling.” Producer **Kim Hyun‑soo** told Soompi, “We wanted to push the envelope this season by intertwining personal stories with the larger social commentary that made the first three seasons resonate. The 2.8% rating confirms that fans are hungry for more.”

## Why This Matters

The strong premiere rating signals that **New Recruit** remains a powerhouse in the competitive cable‑drama market, where viewership can fluctuate dramatically with each new season. By achieving a 2.8% rating against a well‑established rival, the series demonstrates the staying power of genre‑blending narratives that marry comedy with social critique.

From an industry perspective, the drama’s success underscores the growing appetite for content that reflects real‑life experiences of South Korean youth, particularly the mandatory military service that affects nearly every male citizen. The inclusion of high‑profile K‑pop figures like IU also illustrates the increasing cross‑pollination between music and television, a trend that streaming services such as Netflix and Disney+ have capitalized on to attract global subscribers.

Moreover, the ratings battle with **My Bias, My Boss**—a romantic comedy that traditionally dominates primetime slots—highlights a shift in audience preferences toward more nuanced storytelling. If **New Recruit 4** maintains or improves its ratings, it could influence network programming strategies, encouraging more black‑comedy or socially aware dramas to be green‑lit for prime cable slots.

## What Fans Can Expect

Following the premiere, ENA has announced that **New Recruit 4** will air weekly on Wednesdays at 21:00 KST for a total of 12 episodes. International fans can look forward to the series becoming available on **Netflix** and **Viki** within two weeks of each Korean broadcast, complete with subtitles in English, Spanish, and French. The network also teased a special behind‑the‑scenes documentary slated for release on **YouTube** after the season finale, offering deeper insight into the production process and the real‑life military consultants who helped shape the script.

In addition, the drama’s soundtrack—curated by renowned composer **Lee Ji‑hoon**—will drop on **Spotify**, **Apple Music**, and **Melon** next Friday, featuring original songs performed by the cast and guest artists. Early teasers suggest a blend of nostalgic military march motifs with contemporary K‑pop beats, promising a chart‑ready album that could make waves on the **Billboard World Albums** chart.
