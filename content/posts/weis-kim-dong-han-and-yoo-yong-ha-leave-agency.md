WEi’s two prominent members, Kim Dong Han and Yoo Yong Ha, have officially ended their exclusive contracts with OUI Entertainment, sending waves through the fandom and industry alike. The agency confirmed the split on August 25, noting the agreement was mutual and coincided with the natural expiration of their contracts.

## Background

Formed in 2020 through the Mnet survival show *Produce X 101*, WEi quickly rose to prominence as a nine‑member boy group under OUI Entertainment. Their debut EP *Identity: First Sight* showcased a blend of powerful choreography and emotive vocals, earning them a spot on the Billboard World Albums Chart and solidifying their presence on Korea’s Circle (formerly Gaon) charts. Over the past three years, WEi has delivered a string of hits—"All About You," "Starry Night," and the recent summer anthem "Bye Bye Bye"—each cementing their reputation for genre‑spanning music and tight performances.

Kim Dong Han, known for his charismatic stage presence and songwriting contributions, has also pursued solo activities, including a soundtrack appearance for the drama *The Devil Judge* and a cameo in the web series *Love Revolution*. Yoo Yong Ha, the group's main vocalist, earned praise for his vocal range on tracks like "All Or Nothing" and has been featured on several variety shows, expanding his individual fanbase beyond WEi’s core supporters.

Despite their success, WEi’s contract cycle with OUI Entertainment was set to end in mid‑2024. The agency’s statement highlighted that both members chose not to renew, a decision that aligns with a broader trend of idols exploring independent paths or new agencies after a standard seven‑year contract.

## What Happened

On August 25, OUI Entertainment released an official notice on its website and social media channels, stating: “We have mutually agreed with Kim Dong Han and Yoo Yong Ha to conclude their exclusive contracts following the expiration of their contract terms.” The announcement was brief, offering no details about the artists’ future plans or whether they intend to remain in the entertainment industry. The agency thanked the two for their contributions and wished them success in their upcoming endeavors.

Fans quickly took to Twitter, Instagram, and Weverse, expressing a mixture of shock, support, and curiosity. Hashtags such as #KimDongHanLeaving and #YooYongHaExit trended worldwide within hours. Several fan‑run accounts compiled timelines of the members’ activities, noting that both had been relatively active in solo projects throughout 2023, hinting at possible individual ambitions beyond the group framework.

No official statements from the members themselves have been released yet, but sources close to the idols suggest they are negotiating new representation and may explore solo music, acting, or even overseas opportunities. The timing also coincides with WEi’s upcoming comeback slated for early 2025, raising questions about how the group will promote as a seven‑member unit.

## Why This Matters

The departure of two key members from a relatively young group like WEi is significant for several reasons. First, it underscores the evolving nature of K‑pop contract dynamics. While the classic seven‑year “golden handcuffs” model still dominates, more idols are opting for flexibility after their initial term, seeking artistic freedom or better financial terms elsewhere. This shift can influence how agencies structure future contracts, potentially offering more profit‑sharing or creative control.

Second, WEi’s brand identity has been built on a balanced distribution of vocal, rap, and dance responsibilities among its members. Losing both a main vocalist and a visual‑lead rapper could affect the group’s sound and performance style. Historically, groups such as EXO and GOT7 have navigated similar member changes, sometimes emerging stronger, but the outcome often hinges on how the remaining members adapt and whether new talent is introduced.

Third, the news impacts fan engagement and market performance. WEi’s recent releases have shown steady growth on the Circle Album Chart, and their streaming numbers on Spotify and Apple Music have been climbing. A sudden lineup change can cause short‑term volatility in chart positions and streaming counts, especially if fans decide to support solo endeavors over group activities. Conversely, successful solo projects can broaden the group’s overall reach and attract new listeners.

## What Fans Can Expect

In the coming weeks, fans should keep an eye on official channels for any announcements regarding Kim Dong Han and Yoo Yong Ha’s next steps. If they sign with new agencies, we can anticipate teaser images, solo singles, or perhaps collaborations with other artists. For WEi, OUI Entertainment has hinted that the group will continue promotions as a seven‑member ensemble, with a comeback planned for early 2025. Expect concept photos and a possible re‑branding that highlights the remaining members’ strengths.

Additionally, the fan community is likely to organize streaming parties and support campaigns to maintain WEi’s momentum on Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales chart and the Korean Circle charts. Fans may also see increased activity on YouTube, where WEi’s official channel could release behind‑the‑scenes content to keep engagement high during the transition period.
