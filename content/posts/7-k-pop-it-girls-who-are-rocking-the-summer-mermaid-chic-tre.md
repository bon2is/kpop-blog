The summer heat has sparked a wave of whimsical, sea‑themed fashion, and K‑pop’s biggest style icons are at the forefront. From iridescent gowns to pastel‑tinted accessories, seven K‑pop "It Girls" have embraced the mermaid chic trend, proving that the genre’s influence extends far beyond music. Their looks are not only turning heads on Instagram but also setting new standards for seasonal style.

## Background

K‑pop idols have long been fashion trendsetters, thanks to the genre’s high‑budget music videos, elaborate stage costumes, and the close relationship between idols and their stylists. Groups like BLACKPINK, TWICE, and soloists such as IU have historically shaped streetwear, luxury collaborations, and even traditional Korean hanbok reinterpretations. In recent years, nostalgia‑driven aesthetics—think 90s Y2K vibes and retro pastel palettes—have resurged globally, and the mermaid chic trend is the latest iteration. This look draws inspiration from the mythic allure of oceanic sirens, combining flowing silhouettes, shimmery fabrics, and a palette of sea‑foam greens, coral pinks, and iridescent blues.

The trend aligns perfectly with the current fashion climate, where sustainability and escapism intersect. Fans are looking for outfits that feel both dreamy and wearable, and K‑pop idols are delivering by partnering with designers who prioritize eco‑friendly fabrics while still achieving that luminous, underwater glow. As summer festivals and beach concerts approach, the mermaid aesthetic has become a go‑to choice for idols wanting to stand out in a sea of bright summer colors.

## What Happened

Earlier this month, Soompi highlighted seven K‑pop idols who have already made waves with mermaid chic looks. The list includes BLACKPINK’s Lisa, who debuted a holographic, scale‑patterned mini dress during a promotional event in Seoul; TWICE’s Nayeon, who wore a pastel‑blue chiffon gown with pearl‑embellished shells at a beachside fan signing; and soloist IU, whose ethereal silver slip dress featured a subtle gradient reminiscent of sunrise over the ocean, spotted during a live‑streamed concert.

Other notable mentions are EXO’s Kai, who rocked a teal satin bomber jacket paired with iridescent cargo pants at a fashion show; Red Velvet’s Irene, whose mermaid‑inspired hair accessories—tiny seashell hairpins and a pearl‑studded headband—completed her pastel lilac dress at a charity gala; and (G)I‑DLE’s Soyeon, who turned heads in a neon‑green mesh top layered over a metallic fish‑scale skirt at a music festival. Finally, NCT’s Taeyong was seen in a sleek, navy‑blue velvet coat with subtle scale embossing during a backstage interview for a variety show.

All seven idols were photographed by top fashion photographers, and the images quickly trended on Twitter and Instagram, with fans reposting and recreating the looks. Stylists involved in these ensembles emphasized the importance of texture—mixing fabrics like organza, silk, and sequins—to achieve the fluid, underwater effect. While no official brand collaborations were announced, several of the outfits featured pieces from emerging Korean designers known for avant‑garde, sea‑inspired collections.

## Why This Matters

The mermaid chic surge underscores K‑pop’s role as a global fashion catalyst. Historically, K‑pop’s visual concepts have driven sales for brands ranging from luxury houses like Chanel and Dior to fast‑fashion giants such as Zara and H&M. By adopting a niche aesthetic like mermaid chic, idols are expanding the palette of trends that Korean pop culture can export. This also reflects a broader shift toward thematic, story‑driven styling in K‑pop, where concepts are not limited to a single music video but spill over into everyday public appearances.

From an industry standpoint, the trend offers lucrative opportunities for Korean designers and textile manufacturers. The demand for iridescent fabrics and custom‑crafted accessories has spiked, prompting local manufacturers to invest in sustainable, recycled materials that mimic the sparkle of the sea without harming marine ecosystems. Moreover, the visibility of these looks on global platforms—Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube—means that international fans are likely to seek out similar pieces, driving cross‑border e‑commerce sales.

Chart-wise, the idols involved are already dominating Billboard’s Global 200 and Korea’s Circle Chart, reinforcing the link between musical success and fashion influence. When a top‑ranking artist like Lisa or IU adopts a new style, it often translates into increased streaming numbers and higher engagement rates, as fans search for “how to get the look” or “where to buy” the featured items. This synergy amplifies both music and merchandise revenue streams.

## What Fans Can Expect

Looking ahead, fans can anticipate more mermaid‑inspired moments across upcoming comebacks and world tours. BLACKPINK is slated to release a summer single in September, and early teasers suggest a continuation of the aquatic theme, potentially featuring a full‑length music video set underwater. TWICE’s next EP is rumored to include a beach‑vibe concept, which could see Nayeon and the other members expanding on their pastel‑blue aesthetics with coordinated stage outfits.

Beyond music, several K‑pop fashion brands are planning limited‑edition capsule collections that channel mermaid chic, offering everything from scale‑patterned streetwear to pearl‑adorned accessories. Fans should keep an eye on official brand announcements and pre‑order windows, which often open a week or two before the idols are photographed in the looks.
