The K‑pop world was set ablaze this week when an NCT fan event in Seoul unveiled a surprise merchandise auction that included clothing once owned by former member Taeil, who was convicted of sexual assault in 2022. Fans and netizens alike flooded social media with accusations of negligence, demanding swift action from SM Entertainment and the group’s management.


## Background

NCT, short for Neo Culture Technology, debuted under SM Entertainment in 2016 with a unique concept of unlimited members divided into multiple sub‑units such as NCT 127, NCT Dream, and WayV. Over the past eight years the collective has amassed a global following, topping Billboard's World Albums chart with releases like *NCT 2020 Resonance* and breaking into the Billboard 200 with *NCT 2021 Universe*. Their intricate choreography, genre‑bending music, and multilingual outreach have secured them spots on major streaming platforms, including Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube, where their videos routinely rack up hundreds of millions of views.


The group’s lineup has shifted frequently, with members rotating in and out as part of SM’s experimental model. In 2020, vocalist Lee Tae‑il (known simply as Taeil) was expelled from the agency after a court found him guilty of sexual assault against a former girlfriend. The verdict, delivered by the Seoul Central District Court, sentenced him to a two‑year prison term, later reduced to a suspended sentence on appeal. SM Entertainment issued a brief statement confirming his removal and emphasizing a zero‑tolerance policy for illegal behavior.


Since his departure, NCT has continued to thrive. Their most recent comeback, the *Glitch Mode* EP by NCT Dream, debuted at number one on the Circle Album Chart and secured a top‑10 spot on the Billboard 200, marking a historic crossover for the sub‑unit. The group’s worldwide fanbase, known as NCTzens, remains active across platforms like Twitter, Reddit, and Discord, often coordinating streaming parties to boost chart performance.


## What Happened

On August 31, 2026, SM Entertainment hosted a “NCT Fan Appreciation Day” at the COEX Convention Center in Seoul. The event featured a live performance, a Q&A session with members of NCT 127 and NCT Dream, and a charity auction of limited‑edition items donated by the idols themselves. According to the official event recap posted on SM’s YouTube channel, the auction included handwritten lyric sheets, custom‑designed apparel, and personal accessories.


Mid‑way through the livestream, a fan named @KPopWatcher on Twitter flagged a photo of a black bomber jacket being presented for bid. The jacket bore a small, embroidered tag with the name “Taeil” inside the lining. Within minutes, the clip went viral, prompting a flood of comments demanding an explanation. SM’s community manager responded within the hour, stating: “We regret any oversight regarding the provenance of auction items. The jacket was unintentionally included and will be removed from the sale immediately.”


Further investigation revealed that the jacket had been part of a donation pile submitted by current member Johnny, who mistakenly included a piece from the group’s early rehearsal wardrobe that had once been worn by Taeil during his tenure. No other items linked to Taeil were identified, but the incident sparked a broader conversation about how agencies handle memorabilia tied to disgraced former members.


## Why This Matters

The controversy hits at several sensitive points in K‑pop’s evolving landscape. First, it underscores the difficulty agencies face in managing the legacy of former idols who have committed crimes. While SM quickly terminated Taeil’s contract, the residual presence of his belongings in the group’s archives demonstrates a gap in internal vetting processes. Fans expect agencies to exercise rigorous due diligence, especially when charitable auctions are involved.


Second, the incident amplifies the growing power of fan activism online. In the past, scandals were often contained within Korean media, but today a single tweet can mobilize a global audience, pressuring companies to act swiftly. The rapid response from SM—removing the item and issuing an apology—shows how agencies must be prepared for real‑time crisis management.


Third, the episode may have commercial repercussions. NCT’s brand partnerships with global platforms such as Spotify and YouTube rely on a clean image. Any perception of insensitivity could jeopardize sponsorships or streaming playlist placements. Moreover, the incident arrived just weeks before the group’s planned Asian tour, raising concerns that ticket sales could be affected if fans choose to boycott.


## What Fans Can Expect

SM Entertainment has announced that the auction will continue without the contested jacket, and proceeds from the remaining items will be donated to a Korean women’s shelter organization. In addition, the agency pledged to conduct an internal audit of all memorabilia slated for future events, ensuring no items linked to former members with criminal records are inadvertently included.


Looking ahead, NCT is slated to release a new digital single on September 20, 2026, accompanied by a music video that will debut on YouTube and be available for streaming on Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music. The group will also embark on the “NCT 2026 World Tour” starting in November, with tickets expected to go on sale through Ticketmaster and local Korean ticketing platforms like Interpark. Fans eager to attend should keep an eye on official channels for exact dates and venue details.
