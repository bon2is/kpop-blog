Noh Seul Bi, the former shaman turned TV personality, has ignited a fresh conversation about body image and fashion after publicly responding to criticism of her revealing outfits. In a candid Instagram Live on August 20, the 31‑year‑old star defended her style choices, sparking a flurry of comments, memes, and heated debates across Korean and global fan communities.

## Background

Noh Seul Bi first entered the public eye as a traditional shaman, known for her spiritual consultations and appearances on local TV programs. Her breakthrough came in 2022 when she joined the popular variety show "SNL Korea" as a recurring guest, where her quick wit and unapologetic humor won over a younger audience. Since then, she has transitioned into a full‑time celebrity, signing with a major entertainment agency and appearing on talk shows, music videos, and fashion events.

Her rapid rise mirrors the trajectory of other non‑idol personalities who have leveraged variety‑show fame into broader media careers, such as comedian Yoo Jae‑suk and actress Kim So‑hyun. While Noh’s background is rooted in shamanistic traditions, she has embraced the glossy aesthetics of K‑pop culture, often collaborating with stylists known for dressing top idols like BLACKPINK’s Jennie and BTS’s J‑Hope. Her recent fashion choices—skin‑tight dresses, sheer tops, and high‑cut skirts—have drawn both admiration and backlash, reflecting the polarised views on body positivity in South Korea.

## What Happened

On August 20, Noh Seul Bi took to Instagram Live to address a wave of negative comments that had surfaced after she posted a series of photos in a sheer, sequined outfit for a brand endorsement. Critics accused her of “over‑sexualizing” herself and disrespecting traditional Korean modesty. In response, Noh calmly explained that her clothing choices were a form of self‑expression and not meant to offend. She quoted the famous Korean proverb, “옷은 사람을 만든다” (clothes make the person), emphasizing that fashion is a personal narrative.

During the live stream, Noh cited Jennie of BLACKPINK as an inspiration, noting that many fans admire Jennie’s confidence in similar outfits. She said, “I want a body like Jennie’s, but more importantly, I want the confidence to wear what makes me feel alive.” The stream quickly garnered over 150,000 views, with many fans flooding the chat with supportive messages, while detractors continued to voice disapproval.

The incident was covered by Korean entertainment sites such as Koreaboo and Naver, and it quickly trended on Twitter under the hashtag #NohSeulBiResponse. International fans also chimed in, with English‑speaking K‑pop communities sharing the clip on YouTube and Reddit, further amplifying the discussion.

## Why This Matters

Noh Seul Bi’s public defense underscores a broader cultural clash in South Korea: the tension between traditional values and the increasingly global, body‑positive aesthetics championed by K‑pop idols. While idols like BLACKPINK and BTS have long pushed fashion boundaries, they are backed by massive agency resources and carefully curated branding. Noh, however, is navigating this space without the same level of institutional support, making her stance a litmus test for how independent personalities can claim agency over their image.

The debate also touches on the #MeToo‑era discourse in Korean entertainment, where female celebrities are often scrutinized more harshly for their appearance than their male counterparts. By confronting criticism head‑on, Noh adds her voice to a growing chorus of women demanding respect for personal style choices, echoing statements from artists such as IU and HyunA who have spoken out against body shaming.

From an industry perspective, the incident could influence how brands approach collaborations with non‑idol personalities. Companies may become more cautious, ensuring that marketing campaigns align with cultural sensitivities while still embracing modern aesthetics. Conversely, Noh’s surge in support demonstrates a market for bold, confidence‑driven fashion that resonates with younger, global fans.

## What Fans Can Expect

Following the live stream, Noh Seul Bi announced that she will be partnering with a Korean street‑wear label for a limited‑edition capsule collection slated for release in late September. The line is expected to feature the same daring silhouettes she defended, allowing fans to purchase pieces that mirror her style. While exact retail details are pending, the brand hinted at availability on major Korean e‑commerce platforms like WConcept and possibly on global sites such as Farfetch.

Additionally, Noh hinted at a forthcoming appearance on a new reality‑show pilot focused on “modern spirituality meets pop culture,” set to air on a streaming platform later this year. Though the platform has not been confirmed, industry insiders suggest it could land on Netflix or a Korean OTT service like TVING, providing international fans another avenue to follow her journey.
