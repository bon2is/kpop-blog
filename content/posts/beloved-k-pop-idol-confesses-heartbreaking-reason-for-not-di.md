Soyou, the former SISTAR vocalist beloved for her soulful voice and gentle demeanor, surprised fans on August 24th with a raw video titled “Everything Revealed About Soyou’s…” in which she admits she’s gaining weight and, more importantly, shares the heartbreaking reason she stopped dieting. The confession quickly went viral, igniting discussions about body‑image pressures in K‑Pop and the importance of mental health.

## Background

Born Kang Ji‑yeon in 1992, Soyou debuted as the lead vocalist of the iconic girl group SISTAR in 2010. The quartet—Sistar, Hyolyn, and Dasom—dominated the Korean charts with hits like “Alone,” “Touch My Body,” and “I Like That,” earning multiple Daesang awards and cementing their status as one of the most successful summer‑season groups in K‑Pop history. After SISTAR’s disbandment in 2017, Soyou embarked on a solo career, delivering emotive ballads such as “Good Night,” “You,” and the chart‑topping duet “Some.” Her mellow vocal tone and relatable personality have kept her a fan‑favorite, especially on variety shows where she often speaks candidly about life beyond the stage.

In recent years, Soyou has been active on streaming platforms—her solo releases are available on Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music, while her older SISTAR catalog remains on all major services. She also appears regularly on Korean reality programs like “My Little Television” and “Begin Again,” where she showcases her songwriting skills and genuine connection with audiences.

## What Happened

On August 24th, Soyou posted a 5‑minute video on her official YouTube channel, titled “Everything Revealed About Soyou’s…” The video opens with her sitting in a softly lit room, looking directly into the camera. She first acknowledges that she’s been gaining weight over the past few months, a fact that fans had already begun to notice on her Instagram posts. Rather than offering a typical “I’m fine” response, Soyou delves into the emotional catalyst behind her decision to stop dieting.

She explains that a close friend—who had struggled with an eating disorder—confided in her about the devastating impact of restrictive diets on mental health. The conversation hit home, reminding Soyou of the relentless scrutiny she faced during her SISTAR days, where every meal was monitored by agencies, stylists, and fans alike. “I realized I was living for other people’s expectations, not my own health,” Soyou says, her voice trembling slightly. She adds that the turning point came when she missed a scheduled live broadcast because she felt too exhausted from constant calorie counting. That day, she decided to prioritize her wellbeing over aesthetic standards.

Soyou also shares that she’s now focusing on a balanced diet and regular exercise for health, not weight loss. She encourages fans to love themselves as they are, stating, “Your worth isn’t measured by a number on a scale.” The video ends with a heartfelt thank‑you to supporters who have sent encouraging messages, and a promise to be more open about her journey moving forward.

## Why This Matters

Soyou’s revelation is significant for several reasons. First, it underscores the ongoing issue of body‑image pressure in K‑Pop, an industry historically known for its strict aesthetic standards. While male idols have begun speaking out about mental health, female idols remain under intense scrutiny, often forced to maintain a “perfect” figure to meet fan and agency expectations. Soyou’s candidness adds to a growing chorus of voices—including BTS’s Jin, who discussed his own struggles with body image, and former Girls’ Generation member Sunny, who opened up about eating disorders—highlighting a shift toward transparency.

Second, Soyou’s decision to stop dieting and prioritize health can influence younger idols and trainees who look up to her. Her platform reaches millions globally; by sharing her story on YouTube, she ensures the message is accessible to international fans on platforms like YouTube and Spotify, where her music streams.

Finally, the video’s viral spread demonstrates the power of personal storytelling in the digital age. Within 24 hours, the clip garnered over 2 million views, trending on Twitter with hashtags #SoyouConfession and #BodyPositivity. Media outlets from Korea to the United States picked up the story, further amplifying the conversation about mental health in the entertainment industry.

## What Fans Can Expect

Following the video, Soyou hinted at upcoming projects. She mentioned working on a new single slated for release later this year, which she describes as “a song about self‑acceptance and healing.” While no official release date has been announced, fans can expect the track to drop on major streaming services such as Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube Music. Additionally, Soyou confirmed she will appear on a special episode of “Begin Again” in September, where she will perform the unreleased song live for the first time.

For those eager to support her, Soyou’s official merchandise—including a limited‑edition “Love Yourself” T‑shirt—will be available on her online store starting next week. The proceeds, she says, will go to a Korean mental‑health charity that assists individuals dealing with eating disorders.
