The latest teaser for Netflix’s highly anticipated thriller **‘Mousetrap’** has set the K‑Drama community abuzz. Featuring Ryu Jun Yeol in a dark, identity‑theft storyline, the stills showcase a chilling scene where a single fingernail becomes the key to the protagonist’s stolen life. Fans are already scrambling to add the series to their watchlists, eager to see how the celebrated actor navigates this tense new role.

## Background

Ryu Jun Yeol burst onto the international scene with his breakout film *"Parasite"* (2019), which won the Palme d’Or and multiple Academy Awards. Since then, he has become one of South Korea’s most versatile actors, seamlessly shifting between romantic comedies like *"Hometown Cha‑Cha‑Cha"* and gritty thrillers such as *"All of Us Are Dead"*. His ability to convey subtle emotion while maintaining a strong screen presence has earned him a dedicated global fanbase and frequent mentions in Billboard’s *World Albums* and *World Digital Song Sales* charts when his projects feature original soundtracks.

In recent years, Ryu has expanded into streaming platforms, starring in Netflix hits that have helped the service dominate the Korean market. His collaboration with Netflix on *"All of Us Are Dead"* broke into the Top 10 on Netflix’s Global Top 10 list, proving his draw for international audiences. Now, with *‘Mousetrap’*, Ryu returns to the streaming giant for a project that blends psychological suspense with a unique visual aesthetic.

The series is adapted from a popular Korean webtoon of the same name, which has amassed millions of reads on platforms like Naver Webtoon. The source material is praised for its intricate plot twists and atmospheric art style—elements that the production team promises to bring to life on screen. While the exact release date remains unconfirmed, Netflix has confirmed that *‘Mousetrap’* will be part of its upcoming fall slate, making it accessible to subscribers worldwide on the platform.

## What Happened

Netflix released a set of new stills today, offering the first visual glimpse of the series. The images feature Ryu Jun Yeol as Moon Jae, a reclusive novelist whose life unravels after a mysterious figure known only as “The Rat” steals his identity, fortune, and even his name. One striking frame shows Moon Jae holding a single fingernail—an unsettling clue that ties the theft to a physical token, hinting at the meticulous, almost obsessive nature of the antagonist.

The drama also introduces Sul Kyung‑Gu as Noja, a tenacious loan shark determined to retrieve what was lost. The chemistry between Ryu and Sul, hinted at in the stills, suggests a cat‑and‑mouse dynamic that will keep viewers on edge. Official statements from Netflix describe the series as a “thrilling exploration of identity, memory, and the lengths one will go to reclaim what’s theirs.” Production is reportedly underway in Seoul, with a mix of indoor studio sets and on‑location shooting to capture the gritty underbelly of the city.

No specific episode count has been disclosed, but typical Netflix Korean thrillers range from 8 to 10 episodes, each running 45‑55 minutes. The series is expected to feature an original soundtrack (OST) from prominent K‑Pop producers, a strategy that has previously boosted soundtrack chart performance on Billboard’s *World Digital Song Sales*.

## Why This Matters

Ryu Jun Yeol’s involvement signals Netflix’s continued investment in high‑profile Korean talent to attract global subscribers. The platform has seen a surge in viewership after launching Korean originals, and *‘Mousetrap’* adds a fresh genre to its roster—psychological thriller—beyond the romantic and fantasy titles that have dominated the market.

The identity‑theft premise taps into a growing appetite for darker, more complex narratives among Western audiences, mirroring the success of series like *“You”* and *“Mindhunter.”* By adapting a well‑known webtoon, Netflix also leverages an existing fanbase, ensuring built‑in buzz and social media traction. Moreover, the series could set new streaming records for Korean thrillers, potentially entering Netflix’s Global Top 10 and influencing future investments in similar adaptations.

From an industry perspective, the collaboration highlights the symbiotic relationship between Korean webtoons and streaming services. Successful adaptations often translate into increased traffic for the original webtoon, boosting revenue for creators and encouraging more cross‑media projects. If *‘Mousetrap’* garners strong viewership, it may pave the way for other niche webtoons to receive high‑budget productions.

## What Fans Can Expect

Fans can look forward to a tightly woven plot that gradually reveals the true nature of “The Rat” while exploring Moon Jae’s psychological descent. Expect atmospheric cinematography, moody lighting, and a soundtrack that blends haunting orchestral pieces with contemporary K‑Pop influences. Ryu’s performance is likely to showcase a darker side of his acting range, offering a contrast to his more light‑hearted roles.

The series will be available exclusively on Netflix, so global viewers can add it to their watchlist now. While the exact premiere date is still under wraps, the platform typically releases Korean dramas weekly, allowing binge‑watchers to stay engaged throughout the season. Keep an eye on Netflix’s official social channels for trailer drops and a possible OST preview on Spotify or Apple Music.
