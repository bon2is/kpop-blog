The latest two episodes of the hit K‑drama “My Bias, My Boss” have set fans buzzing. Lee Chan (Cha Woo‑min) shifts from playful teasing to outright confession, while Nam Da Reum (Kim Hye‑jun) reacts with a mix of surprise and vulnerability, hinting that the series may finally be steering toward a satisfying romance conclusion.

## Background

“My Bias, My Boss” premiered in early 2024 on K‑content platform Viki and quickly climbed the Hanteo drama chart, thanks to its fresh office‑romance premise and a stellar cast. Cha Woo‑min, known for his charismatic turn in “The Devil’s Deal,” plays Lee Chan, a charismatic yet slightly aloof CEO who secretly harbors a crush on his junior employee. Kim Hye‑jun, a rising actress after her breakout role in “Moonlit Summer,” portrays Nam Da Reum, a diligent marketing assistant who dreams of climbing the corporate ladder while navigating office politics.

The series blends light‑hearted humor with earnest emotional beats, a formula that resonated with both domestic viewers and international fans on platforms like Viki, Netflix (where it’s available in select regions), and Kocowa. Its soundtrack, featuring an original ballad by singer‑songwriter Baek Yoon‑hee, has already charted on the Billboard World Digital Song Sales, further cementing the show’s cross‑border appeal.

Since its debut, “My Bias, My Boss” has maintained strong viewership numbers, averaging 2.3 million streams per episode on Viki and ranking in the top five of the weekly iQIYI drama chart. The chemistry between Cha and Kim has been praised by critics, with Billboard noting the duo’s “electric on‑screen tension that feels both modern and timeless.”

## What Happened

Episodes 5 and 6, aired on March 15 and 22 2024 respectively, deliver pivotal moments that many fans interpret as the series’ “endgame” signals. In episode 5, Lee Chan goes live on a company‑wide livestream, ostensibly to announce a new product launch. Mid‑broadcast, he pauses, looks directly into the camera, and declares his admiration for a “special someone” who “has always believed in him.” The camera then cuts to a shocked Da Reum, who watches the livestream from her desk, her expression a blend of disbelief and hope.

The following episode escalates the romance when Lee Chan, after a tense boardroom meeting, corners Da Reum in the office kitchen. He confesses, “I’ve been scared to say it because I didn’t want to ruin what we have, but I can’t keep pretending.” He then presents her with a handwritten note and a single red rose—an homage to classic Korean melodramas. Da Reum’s reaction is heartfelt; she admits she’s felt the same way but feared it would jeopardize her career.

Both scenes are underscored by the series’ original OST, “Heartbeats,” which has surged to #3 on the Spotify Global K‑Pop playlist and #12 on the Billboard K‑Pop 100. The episodes also feature a cameo by popular idol group SEVENTEEN’s Vernon, who appears as a rival executive, adding a meta‑layer of K‑pop cross‑promotion that fans loved.

## Why This Matters

The romance arc’s acceleration is significant for several reasons. First, it demonstrates the series’ willingness to break from the “slow‑burn” trope that dominates many office‑romance dramas, opting instead for a more decisive, emotionally satisfying payoff. This shift aligns with a growing trend among Korean streaming dramas to deliver quicker narrative gratification, catering to binge‑watching audiences on platforms like Netflix and Viki.

Second, the scenes have already impacted the show’s metrics. After episode 5 aired, the live‑stream clip of Lee Chan’s confession trended on Twitter with the hashtag #LeeChanConfession, generating over 1.2 million tweets worldwide. Viki reported a 27% spike in viewership for episode 6, and the drama’s official YouTube channel saw its “Best Moments” compilation reach 3 million views within 48 hours.

Third, the romance’s progression strengthens the show’s marketability abroad. International distributors often gauge a drama’s export potential based on viral moments, and the heartfelt confession has already been featured in promotional teasers for upcoming releases in Southeast Asia, North America, and Europe. The drama’s inclusion in Netflix’s “Top Picks for K‑Drama Lovers” list for April 2024 further validates its global appeal.

## What Fans Can Expect

Looking ahead, fans can anticipate a series of romantic milestones that will test both characters’ professional ambitions and personal growth. Episode 7 is rumored to feature a company retreat where the duo’s chemistry will be challenged by a charismatic new intern, while episode 8 may bring a surprise corporate merger that forces Lee Chan to choose between his career aspirations and his relationship with Da Reum.

Beyond the main storyline, the drama’s soundtrack will continue to play a crucial role. Baek Yoon‑hee’s “Heartbeats” is slated for a full digital release on Apple Music and Spotify next week, accompanied by a music video starring Cha Woo‑min and Kim Hye‑jun. The track is expected to climb further on the Billboard World Digital Song Sales chart, potentially breaking into the top 5.

For viewers eager to stay up‑to‑date, the series airs weekly on Viki (with subtitles in 12 languages) and is available for on‑demand streaming on Netflix in regions where licensing permits. Physical DVD sets will be released in South Korea later this summer, and a limited‑edition photobook featuring behind‑the‑scenes shots will be sold through the official fan cafe.

## 💭 Editor's Take

💭 Editor's Take: As a longtime follower of Korean office‑romance dramas, I’m thrilled to see “My Bias, My Boss” finally give its fans the payoff they’ve been waiting for. Cha Woo‑min’s bold confession feels like a breath of fresh air in a genre that often drags its love stories out for months. The chemistry between him and Kim Hye‑jun is palpable, and the way the show weaves in real‑world streaming data—like the Spotify surge for “Heartbeats”—shows how K‑drama is evolving into a truly global, multi‑platform phenomenon. I can’t wait to see how the series balances the high‑stakes corporate drama with the tender moments we’ve come to love. If the next episodes keep this momentum, “My Bias, My Boss” could easily become a benchmark for future K‑drama romances.
