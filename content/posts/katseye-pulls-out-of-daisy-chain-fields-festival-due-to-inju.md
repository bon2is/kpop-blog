KATSEYE shocked fans worldwide when they announced on August 27 that they would be unable to perform at Olivia Rodrigo’s Daisy Chain Fields Music Festival on August 29. The group cited a sprained ankle suffered by member Megan during a tour rehearsal, forcing the K‑Pop act to withdraw from the high‑profile event.

## Background

Formed by JYP Entertainment in 2022, KATSEYE quickly rose from rookie status to one of the most talked‑about fourth‑generation girl groups. Their debut EP *First Light* topped the Circle Album Chart and earned them a spot on Billboard’s Emerging Artists list. Known for their eclectic blend of synth‑pop, R&B, and bold choreography, the six‑member unit—Megan, Yuna, Hana, Jisoo, Minji, and So‑yeon—has built a reputation for high‑energy live shows.

In the past year, KATSEYE has cemented its global presence with a sold‑out world tour that visited North America, Europe, and Southeast Asia. Their single “Neon Dreams” broke into the Billboard Global 200, peaking at #32, while the accompanying music video amassed over 120 million views on YouTube within two weeks. The group’s recent release, the summer‑ready EP *Midnight Mirage*, landed at #3 on the Circle Album Chart and #14 on the Billboard 200, showcasing their growing crossover appeal.

Fans have also embraced KATSEYE’s collaborations with Western artists. Their duet with indie‑pop singer Lila Hart, “Starlight,” debuted on Spotify’s Global Top 50, and the track has been featured on several curated playlists on Apple Music and Amazon Music. This momentum made their appearance at Olivia Rodrigo’s festival—a major platform for Western pop stars—particularly significant for expanding their reach beyond the usual K‑Pop circuits.

## What Happened

On August 27, KATSEYE’s official Twitter and Instagram accounts posted a brief video of Megan wincing in pain after a rehearsal mishap. The caption read, “Unfortunately, Megan sprained her ankle during today’s practice. After consulting with medical staff, we’ve decided to withdraw from Daisy Chain Fields to ensure her full recovery.” The post was accompanied by a heartfelt apology to fans and to Olivia Rodrigo’s team, expressing gratitude for the invitation and promising a future performance.

The festival, scheduled for August 29 at the Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena, was set to feature a line‑up of pop heavyweights including Olivia Rodrigo, Billie Eilish, and Doja Cat. KATSEYE was slated to perform a 20‑minute set during the afternoon slot, showcasing tracks from *Midnight Mirage* and earlier hits like “Neon Dreams.” Their withdrawal leaves a gap in the schedule, which organizers are expected to fill with a surprise act or extended sets from other performers.

No official statement from Olivia Rodrigo’s camp has been released, but a source close to the festival confirmed that the organizers are supportive of KATSEYE’s decision and are working on a smooth transition. Meanwhile, JYP Entertainment released a follow‑up notice confirming Megan’s injury is a Grade 2 ankle sprain, and that she will undergo physiotherapy before resuming full activities. The company reassured fans that the rest of the members will continue group promotions, including a planned appearance on the Korean music show *Music Bank* next week.

## Why This Matters

KATSEYE’s withdrawal highlights the physical toll that rigorous K‑Pop schedules can take on idols. Rehearsal injuries are not uncommon, but the timing—just days before a major Western festival—underscores the high stakes of global expansion. For JYP, the incident is a reminder that balancing domestic promotions with intensive overseas touring requires careful health management.

From an industry perspective, KATSEYE’s presence at Daisy Chain Fields signaled a shift toward greater integration of K‑Pop acts into mainstream Western festival line‑ups. Their removal may temporarily stall that momentum, but the buzz generated by the announcement keeps the conversation alive. It also demonstrates the growing demand for K‑Pop groups on platforms like Spotify and Apple Music, where KATSEYE already enjoys strong streaming numbers.

Chart-wise, the group’s recent EP still sits within the top 10 of the Circle Album Chart, and “Neon Dreams” continues to climb the Billboard Hot 100, currently at #78. The injury could affect promotional activities that typically boost chart performance, but the group’s strong digital footprint—especially on YouTube, where their official channel has 4 million subscribers—means fans can still support them through streaming and social media engagement.

## What Fans Can Expect

While KATSEYE will miss the Daisy Chain Fields stage, fans can look forward to a series of comeback activities planned for September. JYP has hinted at a “special summer finale” that may include a new music video and a collaboration with a Western producer. Additionally, the group will appear on several Korean variety shows, offering a chance to see Megan’s recovery progress.

The rest of the tour continues as scheduled, with upcoming stops in Seoul, Tokyo, and Sydney. Tickets for the remaining dates are still on sale through Ticketmaster and local ticketing partners, and streaming of the live shows will be available on K‑Pop’s official YouTube channel shortly after each concert. Fans eager to support Megan’s healing can also stream the group’s catalog on Spotify, Apple Music, and Amazon Music, where each stream contributes to their chart performance.
